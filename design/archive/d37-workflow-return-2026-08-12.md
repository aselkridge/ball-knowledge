FINDINGS 1 · THE RESPONSE ECONOMY (what the passive side gets during a whole-team turn)

GAME: Blood Bowl (all editions, tabletop and BB2/BB3 digital) · PROBLEM: what to give the passive side during a full 16-piece turn. Its answer: nothing on the board, compensated by the turnover threat, since any failed action ends the active turn instantly. · HOW: pure IGO-UGO. The defending coach takes zero actions; their only response is the standing chance the offense trips over its own dice. The community's own measurement of the result: the receiving team dictates the whole drive, per BBTactics' turn-structure essay, "the coach that receives the kick off gets to generally dictate the flow of the game... the longer they take to score a touchdown the less turns the opposing coach gets," and the proposed fix (BB54) restructures drives rather than adding reactions (https://bbtactics.com/bb54/). Player-facing, both sides of the argument in one Steam thread: user Butter Bot, "instead of having 1 player downed, you have 11 players that can't play... a bad luck mean that you skip your turn wich is frustrating"; Grimmsch (playing since 1988) calls turnover "THE core rule" that keeps games fast (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1016950/discussions/0/4766584846449989531/). · VERDICT: ADAPT the turnover half, REJECT the nothing half. A defense can be paid in risk imposed on the offense instead of moves, but zero on-board response reads as passivity, the exact "feels unfair" already rejected. THIN: verification lens could not open the rulebook or these threads this session; quotes stand on the lane's fetches.

GAME: DreadBall (Mantic, 1st/2nd ed) · PROBLEM: 5-action Rushes left the passive team watching; Mantic added exactly one interrupt. · HOW: "Run Interference," the one way the non-active team acts mid-Rush: one defending Jack reacts, moving only one space before a Slam, and if the interference strips the ball "the opposing team's Rush is over." One interrupt, one-space cap, Rush-ending payoff. SNIPPET-ONLY: mechanics come from search snippets of https://www.manticgames.com/news/whats-changed-dreadball-second-edition/ and https://www.adepticon.org/wpfiles/2016/NewDreadballFAQ.pdf; no page with transcribed rules text was successfully fetched. · VERDICT: REJECT the timing, ADOPT the sizing. A mid-turn interrupt asks the defender to tap during the offense's input, poison on mobile and fatal for async. But the shape (exactly one defender, one-square cap, big payoff) independently validates the scale under review.

GAME: Kill Team 2024 (Games Workshop) · the closest shipped match to the mechanic under review · PROBLEM: when one side runs out of activations, the other gets a long unanswered run of moves, the exact "whole floor sets up against me" situation. · HOW: "Counteract." Between each remaining enemy activation, the out-of-activations player takes one free 1AP action with an expended operative, hard-capped: "that operative cannot move more than 2''... this takes precedence over all other rules," once per operative per turning point (https://wahapedia.ru/kill-team3/the-rules/core-rules/; design intent at https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/2hZROZgi/these-smart-activation-rules-make-your-kill-team-clashes-more-tactical-and-reactive/). Key properties: fires AFTER the enemy action resolves, small and distance-capped, one response per enemy action, and fully informed, you see everything before you counteract. · VERDICT: ADOPT. This is the drafted Ball Knowledge rule already shipped and accepted in the most-played skirmish game of the moment; it reads as fair not because it matches volume but because it moves last with full information. THIN: the 2-inch cap and once-per-operative wording rest on a single Wahapedia cite the verification pass could not independently confirm.

GAME: Guild Ball (Steamforged) · PROBLEM: avoided the question by never granting a team turn. · HOW: strict alternating single-model activations (https://guild-ball.fandom.com/wiki/Turn_Sequence); the strategic currency becomes activation order, holding a model back until the opponent commits (https://www.tabletopbattles.com/guild-ball-review-part-1/, snippet-level). · VERDICT: REJECT as structure (alternating possessions are locked, and per-piece alternation multiplies taps per beat), but keep as the negative-space proof: every game that achieved move-for-move parity did it by abolishing the team turn. Since Ball Knowledge keeps the team turn, parity must come from something other than matched movement.

NEGATIVE RESULT: no turn-based sports title (Blood Bowl 2/3, DreadBall digital) gives the defense mid-turn tap-to-interrupt input on mobile or console. Searched Steam forums for BB2/BB3 and Mantic/OnTableTop/TechRaptor coverage of DreadBall digital; Blood Bowl digital ships pure IGO-UGO and Run Interference exists only on the tabletop.

FINDINGS 2 · ONE REACTION VS MIRRORED MOVEMENT ON MOBILE

GAME: XCOM 2 and Space Hulk (the pre-commitment family) · PROBLEM: giving the non-acting side presence during the enemy's move without a turn. · HOW: a unit spends its own-turn action to enter overwatch; when an enemy moves in sight the shot fires automatically at a penalty (total hit chance x0.7, reaction shots excluded from hidden aim assist, https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Overwatch_(XCOM:_Enemy_Unknown)). Space Hulk is the same commitment shape plus a small Command Point pool spendable in the enemy turn (https://steamcommunity.com/app/242570/discussions/0/864976115121409568, https://www.orderofgamers.com/downloads/SpaceHulk3rd4thEd_v1.3.pdf). Player-facing, both failure modes in one thread, "Why is overwatch so bad?": gimmethegepgun, "overwatch creeping across the map is boring and ludicrously effective"; Rosebourne: it was "so effective it's why they started putting all these timers in the first place" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/268500/discussions/0/1743342466031978874). · VERDICT: REJECT pre-commitment as the primary mechanic. The reaction must be weakened or it dominates, and once weakened it feels wasted; and a commitment made blind cannot answer a setup it never saw, which recreates the rejected unfairness on rails. ADAPT later, at most, as a special deny stance set during your own possession at an overwatch-style penalty.

GAME: Into the Breach (on phones via Netflix) · PROBLEM: making a once-per-turn response feel powerful rather than stingy. · HOW: every enemy attack is fully telegraphed on the grid before the player answers with few, small, fully informed moves; the pitch verbatim, "all enemy attacks are telegraphed... allowing you to analyze your opponent's attack and come up with the perfect counter" (https://store.steampowered.com/app/590380/Into_the_Breach/). Session-length praise from a player: playable in "15-30 minutes" coffee-break sessions, "little solo-boardgames/tactical one-offs" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/590380/discussions/0/1728711392726992819). The warning: full information inflates think time, not clock time. A player in the perfect-play thread would "continually start over if I lose any grid" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/590380/discussions/0/1694922980059295294/); a snippet-only quote from another discussion has a player spending "5 minutes per turn with pen and paper." · VERDICT: ADOPT the principle, information is the currency that buys down action-count asymmetry; one informed slide against five revealed offense moves is a better trade than it looks, proven on a phone screen. But under a 15-second beat the defensive answer must have a small legal-option set; one defender, 1 to 2 squares, is right-sized, because an informed reaction with many options imports the paralysis into a timed game.

GAME: Wargroove · PROBLEM: symmetry via mirrored whole-army turns, the Advance Wars shape. · HOW: each side moves every unit, then the other side does the same; the passive player watches turn banners, fog reveals, and per-unit combat animations, "potentially a dozen fights per turn." Player-facing, the "Speed up, Wargroove" thread: "I want slick, clean, fast, responsive game, where I can focus on strategy and not on waiting" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/607050/discussions/0/1778261844038260916/). · VERDICT: REJECT mirrored full movement. Matching the offense's 4 free steps with 4 to 5 defensive moves multiplies the watching load on the side that just acted, every possession, on a phone.

GAME: Hero Academy (mobile async tactics) · PROBLEM: making a multi-move turn survivable on a phone. · HOW: five action points spent freely, but the turn only transmits on an explicit confirm, with a rewind wheel to undo risk-free (https://youarecurrent.com/2012/03/27/review-hero-academy-ios/, https://darkzero.co.uk/game-reviews/hero-academy-pc/; the TouchArcade headline "Bite Size Strategy You Can't Put Down" is snippet-only, page 403s, https://toucharcade.com/2012/01/16/hero-academy-review/). · VERDICT: ADAPT the lesson only. Multi-move turns were tolerable ONLY because of batch-confirm plus rewind, a UI layer that costs exactly the taps a 15-second beat forbids. A single one-defender slide needs no confirm, no rewind, no transmit button; the UI tax is itself an argument for one reaction over several small ones.

GAME: MTG Arena (many small reaction windows) · PROBLEM: letting the non-active player interject at every meaningful moment. · HOW: priority passing offers the reacting player a response window at each phase or action; the client auto-passes when you hold nothing. Players report flow as "unbearable with the match being so slow and unresponsive," with delays "when choosing targets, declaring blockers and attackers." SNIPPET-ONLY: thread not individually fetched (https://steamcommunity.com/app/2141910/discussions/0/3904116162873826270). · VERDICT: REJECT several-small-reactions outright. Interleaving a defensive micro-slide after each off-ball step, up to 4 per possession, rebuilds priority-passing inside a 24-second shot clock.

FINDINGS 4 · THE TELEGRAPH SHAPE (defense commits visibly, offense reads and punishes)

GAME: Into the Breach (again, telegraph side) · PROBLEM: reactive damage felt like unfair dice. · HOW: every enemy commits its attack to specific tiles BEFORE the player's turn; the committed action executes even if the situation changed, which is exactly what makes punishing it feel earned; staleness is fought with fresh spawns and competing objectives (postmortem: https://ubm-twvideo01.s3.amazonaws.com/o1/vault/gdc2019/presentations/Into%20the%20Breach%20Postmortem%20Final.pdf; breakdown: https://atomicbobomb.home.blog/2020/05/17/into-the-breach-enemy-intentions/). · VERDICT: ADAPT. Our shape with sides swapped: commitment by the non-acting side turns the acting side's free setup into a decision. Known cost, it reads as puzzle rather than duel; acceptable here because the opponent is a human whose call varies.

GAME: Slay the Spire · PROBLEM: hidden enemy turns made block-vs-attack blind guesses. · HOW: intent icons telegraph the TYPE and damage of the next action but stay deliberately coarse (a buff icon does not say which buff; "unknown" intents exist), and intents change only via triggers printed on the enemy's visible effects bar (https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Intent). Player-facing proof the honesty rule is load-bearing: in the "Enemies changing intent" Steam thread a player accused the game of cheating, the community answer was verbatim "There is nothing that causes any enemy to change its intent that isn't clearly listed on its effects bar," and the OP conceded (https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/5015307809836759698/). A telegraph the player can catch lying destroys the contract. · VERDICT: ADOPT the honesty rule and the coarseness: a defense call should declare WHAT is covered at zone level, never square-exact endpoints, so the read informs without solving the turn. THIN: the effects-bar quote is load-bearing and rests on a thread the verification pass could not open.

GAME: Tecmo Bowl (anti-pattern: hidden simultaneous call) · PROBLEM it created: both sides pick plays hidden and simultaneous; a correct defensive guess collapses the play. · HOW it degenerates, per player writeups: "basically a game of Rock, Paper, Scissors where the offense picks a run or a pass and the defense tries to guess"; skill becomes metagaming the guesser, not playing football (https://jamesecosby3.medium.com/the-greatest-sports-video-games-of-all-time-maybe-tecmo-super-bowl-for-playstation-1-3a0d30ea283b, https://www.tecmobowl-vs-rbi.com/tecmo-strategy.html). · VERDICT: REJECT hidden-simultaneous. A VISIBLE call converts the coin-flip into a read for the offense and a bluff-free commitment for the defense.

GAME: Madden (coverage shells, the stale-read fix) · PROBLEM: once pre-snap alignment reliably reveals the coverage, good players read it every snap ("if your opponent can already guess what play you're about to run, they will run a counter-play"). · HOW: defense still aligns first and visibly, but gets a bounded DISGUISE tool, a shell showing Cover-2 pre-snap while the called play is man, rotating at the snap; the disguise is a look, never a lie about capability. Offense's counter-counter is route-commit gambles, big when right, costly when wrong (https://www.1v1me.com/blog/madden-26-coverage-shells-custom-stem-zones-route-commit, https://www.operationsports.com/identifying-coverages-pre-snap-in-madden-25-and-college-football-25/). · VERDICT: ADAPT carefully and later: ship the honest telegraph first; if playtests show staleness, add disguise as a scarce earned resource (e.g. bought with a hard question), never free, or it collapses into Tecmo's coin flip.

GAME: Yomi / Sirlin's design test · PROBLEM: bare RPS is unskilled because "every option is functionally the same." · HOW, verbatim: "The first step to making a rock, paper, scissors mechanic interesting is to have way different payoffs for winning with each option," payoffs hard to compute exactly so players use judgment, depth from board-state information density (https://www.sirlin.net/articles/designing-yomi). · VERDICT: ADOPT as the test for any coverage menu: each call must be best against a different offensive shape (paint attack, arc attack, star player, clock burn) with board-dependent payoffs; if two calls are interchangeable, cut one.

GAME: Gloomhaven (anti-pattern: blind pre-commit by the ACTING side) · HOW: players lock both action cards before the monster ability card is revealed (https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1868352/monster-ability-cards, https://www.ultraboardgames.com/gloomhaven/game-rules.php). Whoever commits blind eats the feel-bad. NEGATIVE RESULT stated: searches of BGG and Steam found only rules-clarification threads, no dedicated frustration thread; the claim rests on the rule itself. · VERDICT: the order matters. Defense calls first BY DESIGN and is compensated with commitment power; the offense, committing second, pays by being readable in its setup.

GAME: Hoplite (mobile delivery proof) · HOW: every threat is readable on the board (tap an enemy to see its attack zone, deterministic responses) and the player's whole turn is one tap (https://www.pocketgamer.com/hoplite/review/; ResetEra praise thread snippet-only: https://www.resetera.com/threads/let-me-introduce-you-to-hoplite-small-but-addictive-mobile-rogue-like-turn-based-puzzle-game.94812/). · VERDICT: ADOPT the delivery: a defensive call must be drawn on the court as tinted squares or a shaded zone, never announced in a modal; reading it costs the offense zero taps and no clock.

RECOMMENDATION · what the defense gets, and when

Give the defense two things, one at each end of the possession, and buy fairness with information and risk rather than squares.

1. AT THE HANDOFF, BEFORE the offense's free setup: one visible coverage call, a single tap from a small menu (4 or 5 calls, each best against a different offensive shape per the Sirlin test: protect paint, chase arc, shadow the star, press the ball). The call paints a zone on the court Hoplite-style, no modal, no confirm, zero reading cost. It is honest and coarse (Slay the Spire's two rules): it never lies and it declares what is covered, never square-exact endpoints. The offense then does its free 4-piece setup SEEING the call, so setup becomes read-and-punish (Into the Breach) instead of an unanswered race, and the defense's action lands at the moment it has leverage.

2. AFTER the offense's main action resolves: the drafted slide, exactly one defender, 1 to 2 squares by position, the Kill Team Counteract shape, informed and last-moving, constrained to serve the declared call (it lands inside or toward the zone; the ItB rule that a commitment executes as committed is what makes out-reading it feel earned). One drag on an already-highlighted defender, no confirm dialog, zero added taps on the pacing heart, and a legal-option set small enough to decide inside 15 seconds.

3. THE EQUALIZER IS RISK, not movement (Blood Bowl): the offense's free setup carries turnover exposure. A missed question does not just fail the shot, it strands the setup; the defense keeps its slide, the free steps are spent.

Why this answers the rejected "one defender slides 2": that version was an uninformed reactive crumb answering a whole-team setup. The evidence (ItB praise, XCOM complaints, Kill Team acceptance) says one move is not the problem; blindness and timing were. Informed, last, and paired with a possession-framing call, one slide reads as tactical.

Trade-offs of the recommended shape: (a) the call adds one tap per possession at the handoff, the cheapest possible moment but not zero; (b) stale reads if the offense learns to counter every call perfectly, staged fixes in order: board-dependent payoffs, coarseness, then Madden-style disguise as a scarce resource bought with a hard question, never free; (c) it is unshipped as a combination, the moat search found no proven template, so it needs playtest confirmation against the measured even baseline.

Runner-up: the slide alone, post-resolution, no call (pure Kill Team). Cheaper (zero new taps, one fewer system) and already accepted in the most-played skirmish game, but it leaves the defense with nothing at the moment the offense frames the floor, which is the mechanism behind the original unfairness complaint, and it leans entirely on a 2-inch-cap precedent whose citation is thin. Rejected outright by the evidence: mirrored multi-defender movement (Wargroove's waiting, Guild Ball's abolished team turn), several small reaction windows (MTG Arena drag plus Hero Academy's confirm tax), pure pre-commitment or full automation (XCOM overwatch, Football Tactics & Glory's luck-feeling auto-zones, HLT's "absolute nightmare" delegated defense), and hidden simultaneous calls (Tecmo's coin flip).
