Q4. THE COMPRESSION: HOW SHIPPED GAMES SHRANK THE PLAYBOOK INTO A PICK

1. GAME: Madden NFL 11 (GameFlow). PROBLEM: a 300-play book behind formation-then-play trees made every snap a browse. HOW: an AI coordinator calls one play per snap from a situational gameplan of 10-15 weighted plays per bucket; the player accepts or backs out to the full book; the designed receiver is highlighted red with a one-line tip, turning play-calling "from reading and deciphering a foreign language to following instructions" (opened: https://www.siliconsasquatch.com/blog/2010/09/27/sasquatch-soapbox-how-gameflow-makes-madden-11-accessible). Reception split on suggestion quality: SI called Wii GameFlow "a major bust, with seemingly no concept of real-life game situations" (opened, section headed Madden NFL 10 Wii: https://www.si.com/more-sports/2010/08/31/31game-room-august-roundup); player thread, IndyColtsFan: "And Gameflow -- what the heck? It might be an OK feature but come on!" (opened: https://forums.anandtech.com/threads/thoughts-on-the-madden-2011-demo.2093767/post-30210716). VERDICT: ADAPT as an onboarding highlight only, REJECT as the chooser. The moment a suggestion reads situation-blind, trust dies and players route around the feature.

2. GAME: Madden 24 (Coach Suggestions). PROBLEM: machine-filled suggestion slots going stale. UNVERIFIED, snippet-only: the algorithm "often populates with the same play in every slot"; both sources 403 to lane and both verifiers (unreachable: https://forums.ea.com/discussions/madden-nfl-24-general-discussion-en/coach-suggestions-algorithm-/8793875 and https://old.muthead.com/forums/madden/mut-discussion/1354585-how-reliable-are-coach-suggested-plays). VERDICT: WARN, held thin. Direction of the risk is credible: if a short list is machine-filled, duplication is the failure players report. Our lists should be authored sets, never a ranked query that can collapse to one answer.

3. GAME: Madden NFL Football (3DS launch). PROBLEM: console play-calling does not fit a handheld. HOW: EA shipped a three-tier ladder, GameFlow (auto-selects the best play), Classic (full depth of formations and individual plays), and Arcade, which "simplifies options through a more concentrated playbook" (press release PDF opened and extracted by verifier: https://s204.q4cdn.com/701424631/files/doc_news/2011/03/1/559575.pdf). VERDICT: ADOPT the principle. On small screens EA's own answer was to shrink the list, not the screen. A phone-first game ships the Arcade-sized list as the only list.

4. GAME: Tecmo Bowl (NES). PROBLEM: make play-calling itself the mind-game inside seconds. HOW: four plays per side ("There are four formations to choose from", manual opened: http://www.world-of-nintendo.com/manuals/nes/tecmo_bowl.shtml); "when on defense, a player selects a play based on the anticipation of the offense's choice" and a correct guess collapses the play (Wikipedia opened: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecmo_Bowl; verifier note: the defense-guess mechanic rests on Wikipedia, the manual only says pick "the right defending formation"). VERDICT: ADOPT the size, ADAPT the structure. Four is the proven floor for a seconds-scale pick duel. But Tecmo's guess is hidden and symmetric; our defense picks first and visibly, so the offense's counter must pay graded amounts against the shown setup, never a binary blow-up, or trivial counter-reads win every inbound.

- Slay the Spire / Hades: 1-of-3 is the genre standard for a repeated draft ("You see three cards and must choose one or skip", opened but describing Slay the Spire 2: https://www.spirebuilds.com/guides/understanding-card-rewards; Hades II boons assume three throughout, opened, thin: https://hades-2.game-vault.net/wiki/Boons). AC Atienza's version of the same standard, his own opinion, not Mega Crit's: an obviously-best choice means "we've failed as designers" (opened: https://www.cloudfallstudios.com/blog/2020/11/2/game-design-tips-reverse-engineering-slay-the-spires-decisions).

HCI evidence, all opened:
- Hick's Law: RT = a + b log2(n); each doubling of options adds a constant chunk of decision time; no magic count, minimize and categorize (https://ixdf.org/literature/article/hick-s-law-making-the-choice-easier-for-users).
- Overchoice: "Larger choice sets with a small amount of time results in more regret with the decision," and large sets only become enjoyable with more time; Miller cite, about seven items processed at once. Our 15 seconds is precisely the small-time condition, and regret is poison for a pick the opponent then punishes (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overchoice).
- Practitioner guidance: "Limit each decision step to two or three important choices," with "mobile screens need more reduction, desktops offer space"; a case study cutting a form from eight questions to three cut completion time by about a third (verifier note: questions in a form, a slight loosening when applied to options) (https://www.parallelhq.com/blog/what-hick-s-law).

THE NUMBER RANGE, DEFENDED
- DEFENSE (picks first, pick shown): 3. The roguelike draft standard (Spire, Hades), Battlegrounds' entry tier, and the practitioner mobile ceiling, all opened. Recorded player pressure at 3 is mild in both directions, which is what a correct default looks like. Burgun's tax applies: all three setups must each be right in some game state or the pick decays into a reflex.
- OFFENSE (picks second, seeing the defense): 4, acceptable to 5. The reacting side does a harder read (their list plus the shown setup), and 4 is proven at exactly phone-plus-seconds scale (Clash Royale hand, Tecmo's mutual 4, both opened); 5 is the shipped upper bound for a sub-30-second timed pick (TFT, opened). Do not exceed 5.
- HARD CEILING: 6, shipped only against timers roughly 4x ours (Battlegrounds tier 6 at 60+ seconds, with time complaints already on record at that size). Above reading range (Blitz-style 9, unverified) works only through memorization.
- Total on-screen decision load for the offense (4-5 options plus the 1 shown defensive setup) lands at 5-6 items, at the Miller processing cite and under the overchoice regret threshold for time-pressured picks.

RECOMMENDATION: THE PICK-FLOW FOR BALL KNOWLEDGE

Defense phase: 3 authored setups, all visible at once as full-width tiles, one tap to commit, no confirm step (findings: Spire/Hades/BG 3-standard; ParallelHQ mobile guidance; Legend Bowl all-visible rule). The 3 must be authored sets balanced so each is right somewhere (Burgun), and the payoff table punishes off-the-wall picks so 3 stays honest without growing (Action PC Football).

Offense phase: the committed defensive setup stays pinned and visible at the top of the screen; below it, 4 authored counters (5 only if a mode genuinely needs it) in a single thumb-sized bottom strip, Clash Royale hand shape, all visible at 390px, one tap to commit (findings: Clash Royale 4, Tecmo 4, TFT 5 ceiling, CFB 25's one-tap expansion shape if a default-plus-alternates variant is ever wanted).

Payoffs: graded good/neutral/bad against the shown setup, balanced for perfect information, never a Tecmo binary blow-up (findings: Tecmo adapt, Legend Bowl screenlook note).

Explicitly avoid, each tied to a finding:
- Scrolling or paging anywhere inside the timed pick (Legend Bowl, documented player frustration under auto-select).
- Two-hop trees, pick-a-target-then-pick-a-play (NBA 2K25 three-step flow rejected for phone).
- Machine-ranked suggestion slots in place of authored sets (Madden 24 duplicate-slot warning, unverified but the GameFlow trust collapse is confirmed).
- A chooser that is sometimes absent by role or mode (NBA 2K25 Steam MyCareer thread, reads as a bug).
- Metering or rationing the pick in competitive play (CFB 25's finite Play Selection, rejected for core play).
- A lone suggestion row that is not visibly situation-aware (Madden 11 GameFlow reception).
- List sizes above 5 (TFT/BG envelope plus Hick's and overchoice under 15 seconds).
