1. HORNS (also "A-set") · CONFIRMED (two independent opened sources)
- PROBLEM: guarantees the handler a ball screen the defense cannot shade to, because both bigs offer one from each elbow at once.
- FULL PACKAGE: 1 at the top of the key with the ball. 4 and 5 at the left and right elbows ("high near the arc on each side of the lane"). 2 and 3 in the left and right corners. First action: 1 uses either elbow screen; the opposite big pops to the perimeter when the screener rolls. Pass-entry versions hit an elbow big first (the Liberty's "Get": pass to the elbow, follow for the handoff; "Get" is confirmed two-man vocabulary). Receiver's defender: on-ball defender at the top must pick a side over one of two screens; corner defenders pinned on shooters.
- OFF A DEAD BALL: Coach's Clipboard: Horns "is easy to get into right off an inbounds situation"; no fixed inbounder prescribed. Grid adaptation: baseline inbound by the 3, first receiver the 1 retreating to the top, paint empty as the visual tell (INFERENCE).
- BEST AGAINST: both man and zone; the double elbow screen forces immediate defensive decisions.
- VARIANTS sharing the identical starting shape (one pickable shape, many outcomes, which fits the design rule): Horns Twist, Horns Flare, elbow-handoff "Chase" (all three confirmed in the Basketball Dictionary / Hoops Geek). "Spartan/Barcelona/Sonic" are one-site labels, flavor only.
- ERA/LEAGUE: European import carried into the NBA by D'Antoni, Carlisle and others (opened); current WNBA vocabulary proven by FastModel: Aces "Horns Ghost Flare" (2025 playoffs) and Sky "Horns Elbow Dive Duck In" beside NBA entries. Liberty Horns series (film newsletter, opened). Lynx Horns DHO: SNIPPET-ONLY.
- URLS (opened): coaching site https://www.coachesclipboard.net/HornsOffense.html · coaching/analytics site https://www.thehoopsgeek.com/basketball-horns-offense/ · WNBA film newsletter https://coachmarchart.substack.com/p/new-york-liberty-horns-series · play-diagram library https://playbank.fastmodelsports.com/library/basketball/search?searchString=horns

2. FLOPPY · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: frees the best shooter by starting them under the rim, where denial is impossible, with a choice of exits.
- FULL PACKAGE: shooter (2) directly under the basket. 4 and 5 stacked near one block as a staggered double screen, 3 alone on the other side of the lane (the single-double shape). 1 at the top with the ball. First receiver: the shooter, cutting either right or left around a screen to the wing or corner. Receiver's defender: trailing through the screens; tight trail means curl, shortcut means fade.
- OFF A DEAD BALL: a real Floppy BOB series exists (scout's film breakdown, opened): baseline inbound, two scorers wait at the nail for elbow screens, sprint to wing or corners while the screeners slip to the rim; Cleveland made it a BLOB staple. The five variant names on that page (Floppy Down/Up/Screen-the-Screener/Punch/Flow) are UNVERIFIED, one scout's labels, flavor at most.
- BEST AGAINST: man, for catch-and-shoot; the BOB version beats zones by screening the outside of the zone and slipping.
- URLS (opened): coaching site https://www.coachesclipboard.net/floppy-basketball-plays.html · coaching newsletter https://coachhoops.substack.com/p/floppy-action · scout's film breakdown https://medium.com/@KJ_THE_SCOUT/floppy-baseline-out-of-bounds-series-a2f67f128349

3. SPAIN PICK-AND-ROLL (NBA synonym "Stack") · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: turns a ball screen's 2-on-1 into a 3-on-2 by back screening the roller's defender at the exact moment he must guard the roll.
- FULL PACKAGE: 1 with the ball at the top. 5 at the high post/ball-side elbow stepping up to set the ball screen. 2 (best shooter) at the nail/FT line behind 5, back screens the roller's defender then pops to the top arc. 3 and 4 in the corners. First receiver options: 5 rolling (lob or pocket pass) or 2 popping. Receiver's defender: the roller's defender eats the back screen; if the screener's defender hedges, 2 skips the back screen and just pops. Caveat kept from verification: 2's nail start is diagram-derived, no opened page states it in text (THIN on that one spot, uncontradicted).
- OFF A DEAD BALL: nothing inbound-specific documented; entered from the top in flow. Grid adaptation: baseline inbound by 4, receiver 1 at the top (INFERENCE).
- BEST AGAINST: drop coverage and switching; confusion on the pick and on the screen-the-screener at once.
- ERA: named by Half Court Hoops after the Spanish national team; NBA film across Bucks, Heat, 76ers, Jazz. Scariolo/2016-Olympics attribution: SNIPPET-ONLY.
- URLS (opened): coaching site https://www.coachesclipboard.net/spain-pick-and-roll.html · terminology dictionary https://medium.com/thebasketballactiondictionary/spain-pick-and-roll-516cb068a266

4. FIVE-OUT / "DELAY" · CONFIRMED (name and shape); era anchor corrected
- PROBLEM: empties the paint completely so every drive, cut, and closeout attacks an open rim.
- FULL PACKAGE: five perimeter spots on the NBA three-point line, named exactly: left corner, left wing, top, right wing, right corner. Nobody on a block. Positionless; ball usually enters at the top. Engine is rules, not routes (the cleanest fit in this file for "all movement after setup is the players"): passer basket-cuts and fills the opposite corner, back-cut when denied, drive-and-fill. NBA flavor: Delay is this shape with the ball centered to the trailing 5 at the top; that 5's defender, a center, is stranded on the arc. "In the NBA, 5 Out spacing is commonly called Delay or Five"; D'Antoni naming corroborated by RealGM. The "two corners + two 45s" alignment detail: SNIPPET-ONLY.
- OFF A DEAD BALL: nothing inbound-specific documented; it is a flow/trail offense. Grid adaptation: baseline inbound by the 5 who lifts to the top spot last (INFERENCE).
- BEST FOR: teams lacking height wanting tempo and movement; hard to scout because there are no set passes; poor fit with a dominant post. Clean inverse of Iso, which makes the two a good paired choice.
- ERA (corrected): the spacing-revolution anchor is the 2005 Pistons-Spurs Finals, where Detroit's 102 in Game 4 was the only triple-digit game of the series (the lane's "2004" was an error; the 2.8 threes-per-game centers were league leaders that season, not Finals participants).
- URLS (opened): coaching site https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/5-out-motion-offense/ · analytics blog https://theboxand1.weebly.com/nba-posts/karl-anthony-towns-5-out-offense-and-the-evolution-of-nba-spacing · https://basketball.realgm.com/article/240226/Mike-DAntoni-And-The-Delay-Double-Quicks-Prominence-In-Todays-NBA · NBA film newsletter (free preview only, rest paywalled and unused) https://halfcourthoops.substack.com/p/nba-5-out-offense-delay

5. CHIN (Princeton offense, Chin series) · CONFIRMED name; single opened source for the package (THIN)
- PROBLEM: makes overplaying defense pay by building a backdoor read into every entry.
- FULL PACKAGE: 1-2-2 two-guard front: 1 at the top with the ball, two players at the elbows (the "chin" of the FT-line area), two on the wings. Entry: dribble-weave toward a wing into a handoff. First receiver: the wing taking the handoff. Receiver's defender: expected overplaying the handoff, which triggers the counter, a backdoor cut for a layup. Elbow players then screen and pop. The classic 5-back-screens-the-passer follow-up: SNIPPET-ONLY (Breakthrough page not opened).
- OFF A DEAD BALL: not inbound-specific on the opened page.
- BEST AGAINST: aggressive, denying, pressing man; every defensive decision opens a read.
- URLS: opened, Princeton-specialist coaching site https://coachprincetonbasketball.com/chin-set/ · name confirmed across Breakthrough/BFC/Coach's Clipboard, e.g. https://www.breakthroughbasketball.com/coaching/princeton-chin-plays

6. FLEX · CONFIRMED, and era-stamped 80s
- PROBLEM: manufactures layups and open top-of-key jumpers from a repeating two-screen loop that needs no star.
- FULL PACKAGE: 4-out 1-in: always two players at the top of the key, two on the wings, one on the strong-side low block. Trigger: guard-to-guard pass across the top; the weak-side wing receives the flex screen from the block player and cuts baseline into the paint (the flex cut); then the passer down-screens for the flex screener popping to the top (pick-the-picker). First receiver: the other slot guard at the top; his defender in normal top-side position, then forced to navigate the down screen immediately.
- OFF A DEAD BALL: not inbound-specific in sources. Grid adaptation: baseline inbound by the 4, first receiver the 1 popping to the ball-side wing, 5 ball-side block, 3 weak corner, 2 weak wing/slot (INFERENCE).
- BEST AGAINST: man only; sources warn a simple zone defends it, and switching hurts it. Its predictability is authentic era texture.
- ERA (all confirmed verbatim on the page): invented 1967 by Rene Herrerias at Cal, originally "the Cha Cha", shuffle-derived; Jerry Sloan ran his variation for years with the Jazz; Gary Williams won the 2002 NCAA title with it. Honest note: current vocabulary in high school/college, not an NBA/WNBA call today, EXCEPT as the Horns Flex hybrid the WNBA demonstrably runs (CoachTube lesson "WNBA - Horns Flex", opened).
- URLS (opened): encyclopedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flex_offense · coaching site https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/flex-offense/ · coaching course https://coachtube.com/course_lesson/flex-offense-out-of-the-horns-set/wnba-horns-flex/7783251

7. IVERSON (out of 1-4 High) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: gets your best scorer the ball on the move, because it is very difficult to deny a player crossing the court.
- FULL PACKAGE: begins in 1-4 High: 1 at the top with the ball; 4 and 5 at the two elbows; 2 and 3 on opposite wings at FT-line extended. Signature: one guard cuts wing to wing across two elbow screens; the second guard loops under all three to the opposite wing (or clears to the weak corner in the BFC version). First receiver: the Iverson cutter at the wing, FT-line extended. Receiver's defender: chasing through two elbow screens, arriving late and behind. Flows into side PnR, staggers. Naming caveat kept: "Wedge" is specifically an angled back screen for the future ball-screener, not a synonym for plain side PnR; game copy should respect that.
- 1-4 HIGH base has its own pedigree: flattens the defense, empty rim, ideal for backcuts; Auriemma built UConn's Elbow series from it (college, not WNBA, pedigree).
- OFF A DEAD BALL: not inbound-specific on opened pages.
- BEST AGAINST: denial defense on a star; the base shape punishes wing overplay with backdoors.
- URLS (opened): coaching site https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/iverson-cut-basketball/ · college film newsletter https://briangeisinger.substack.com/p/nc-states-iverson-series · coaching site https://www.coachesclipboard.net/14Stack.html

8. PISTOL (also "21") · CONFIRMED name and package; poor dead-ball fit
- PROBLEM: attacks in the first seconds of the clock, playing a sideline two-man game before the defense is set.
- FULL PACKAGE: always up a sideline, never the middle: 1 brings the ball up one sideline; 2 on that same-side wing; 5 at the top of the arc; 3 and 4 fill the weak-side wing and corner. Entries: dribble (1 dribbles at 2, who screens then receives a flare from 5) or pass (1 hits 2 and follows into a ball screen or handoff). First receiver: 2 on the strong wing; his defender sunk toward the middle in unset transition defense. The "21/Pistol" alias is independently real (courses titled exactly that; Basketball Action Dictionary entry).
- OFF A DEAD BALL: explicitly not applicable; Pistol is early offense. This is a real strike against it for a game whose setups fire at dead balls.
- URLS: opened coaching site https://www.coachesclipboard.net/pistol-action-basketball-plays.html · terminology dictionary https://medium.com/thebasketballactiondictionary/pistol-bed8d9f7ef3c . D'Antoni/Nash origin story: SNIPPET-ONLY.

9. ZIPPER (halfcourt set) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: gets a shooter from the baseline to the top off a ball-side down screen, organizing the offense with his defender trapped underneath.
- FULL PACKAGE: 2 and 3 start low on the baseline at the blocks/low wings; 4 and 5 high. 1 dribbles to either wing ("opens" the zipper); the shooters break high off 4's and 5's down screens (the cut up the lane "closes" it); unique in that the down screen happens on the ball side. First receiver: the zipper cutter at the top/slot; his defender pinned under the down screen, chasing from beneath. Options: cutter's shot, screener's post-up, 1's penetration.
- OFF A DEAD BALL: see the Zipper SLOB package in Q1B, which is the documented inbound form. Spurs zipper SLOBs: SNIPPET-ONLY.
- BEST FOR: freeing good shooters; also produces isolations and post-ups; "contained in almost every NBA team's playbook" per the opened source; the zipper cut is in the FIBA coaching manual.
- URLS: opened coaching site https://hooptactics.net/premium/offense/setoffense/zipper.php · FIBA manual (surfaced for name confirmation; the direct fetch DNS-failed and is filed unreachable) https://wabc.fiba.com/manual/level-3/l3-team/2-offensive-tactics-and-strategies/2-1-common-offensive-sets/2-1-8-zipper/

10. ISO / CLEAR-OUT · era-stamped 90s-2000s; alignment THIN (INFERENCE, no opened diagram source)
- PROBLEM: gets your best scorer a one-on-one with no help defender able to arrive in time.
- FULL PACKAGE (alignment is inference; close this gap before shipping): star at the right wing with the entire right side empty; other four stacked left: 2 left corner, 3 left wing, 4 left slot, 5 left block. Inbound adaptation: baseline inbound left by the 4; first receiver the star at the ball-side wing, defender in his jersey, hand-check-era flavor (INFERENCE).
- ERA ANCHOR (CONFIRMED): before 2001-02, illegal-defense guidelines banned zone help, making the clear-out the halfcourt staple; the defensive three-second rule arrived 2001-02 "in conjunction with the removal of previous illegal defense rules prohibiting zone defenses"; Jordan said he never would have had the career he did against legal zones; Duncan, Garnett, McGrady confirmed zones made scoring harder than in the 90s. Post-up corroboration (CONFIRMED): 76ers from 12.8 post-ups/game (2019-20) to 3.7 (2024-25 season, article Dec 2024); more than half the league posts up under five times a game.
- BEST AGAINST: man with no weak-side help, which is what the old rules guaranteed; the era-true counter is that zones beat it.
- URLS (opened): encyclopedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_three-second_violation · analytics newsletter https://draftstack.substack.com/p/the-lost-art-of-posting-up

11. TRIANGLE · CONFIRMED as vocabulary; verdict: legacy only, not a current call
- Breakthrough Basketball, asked directly about today's NBA: not a primary offense anymore; teams run spacing-heavy PnR and motion, with triangle principles blended in. Kept off the base list on that verdict; ship later as a 90s-era/throwback setup if wanted; its overloaded strong side reads distinctly on a grid.
- FULL PACKAGE for the era version: sideline triangle right: 1 ball-side corner, 2 (trigger) ball-side wing, 5 ball-side block; weak side two-man game: 4 (trail) at the top-of-key slot, 3 (opposite) at the weak-side elbow/mid-post. Entry: 1 hits the wing trigger, whose pass choice starts the read tree. Inbound adaptation: sideline inbound right by the 2, first receiver the 1 at the top (INFERENCE).
- ERA (CONFIRMED with caveat): Barry to Winter to Jackson; Bulls 6 titles plus Lakers 5, per the opened page's "From 1990 to 2010"; the specific year ranges 1991-1998 and 2000-2010 are gloss, historically right but not on that page. Best with a dominant post plus wing star; punishes traps and overloads.

Primary source: compiled coaching playbook PDF (Preheim, 125 pages of diagrams), downloaded and read; page numbers cited. 1. BOX (BLOB) · CONFIRMED (shape second-sourced)
- NAME: Box is the alignment; coaches hang many plays off it. Universal vocabulary.
- PROBLEM: four bodies in a tight square inside the lane make every screen point-blank, and one starting shape hides many plays: the box "gives the inbounder four immediate options" and "works well against both man-to-man and zone" (both verbatim, opened).
- FULL PACKAGE (canonical action, Preheim pg. 19): inbounder 3 (best entry passer), baseline, one step outside the ballside lane line. Alignment: 5 ballside block, 4 weakside block, 1 ballside elbow, 2 weakside elbow. Action: 1 and 5 stagger screen for 2; 2 cuts across the lane and catches at the ballside wing/corner; 5 rolls looking for the lob; 4 ducks in. Receiver's defender: starts inside the box on 2 at the weakside elbow, chases through a two-screen stagger, arrives trailing. Note: the specific per-spot assignments are from the single PDF; the core two-blocks-two-elbows shape is the second-sourced part.
- BEST AGAINST: man is the home use; also workable vs zone.
- URLS (opened): playbook PDF https://upstatefranchisebasketball.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/BLOBS-Playbook-Preheim.pdf (pg. 19-24) · coaching guide https://hoopmentality.com/blogs/basketball/baseline-out-of-bounds-plays-guide-for-coaches · coaching sites https://www.coachlynchbasketball.com/post/special-situations-blob-box-series · https://www.breakthroughbasketball.com/plays/baseline-box-plays · https://www.coachesclipboard.net/OBBoxPlays.html (free portion). WNBA tie (Atlanta Dream box-family elevator BLOB): SNIPPET-ONLY, video blocked.

2. STACK (BLOB) · CONFIRMED (absorbs the dropped "Line"; same alignment, better-attested name)
- PROBLEM: a single-file wall of four makes every defender guard through bodies; sequential splits free the third or fourth player in line for a point-blank catch.
- FULL PACKAGE (Preheim "Stack 2", pg. 17): inbounder 3, baseline ballside, one step outside the lane line. Alignment: single file up the ballside lane line: 4 ballside block (front), 5 behind, 2 behind 5, 1 at the back at the ballside elbow/FT line. Action: 4 pops to the ballside corner and RECEIVES the inbound; 5 clears weakside, 2 cuts to the rim, 1 pops to the top; then reversal 4-to-1 and a second wave of screens. Receiver's defender: plays at the front of the stack between 4 and the ball; the corner pop leaves him pinned inside the wall.
- Absorbed Line-family lob action (Preheim "Line 5", pg. 52, kept as Stack flavor): PG inbounds because the bigs are the receivers; 5 ballside block with 4 stacked below, 3 at the FT line, 2 alone weakside mid-post; 5 fakes toward the ball then wheels around 2's back screen for the LOB AT THE RIM; built-in counter, if x2 helps on the lob, fake and hit 2 for the layup. The action is real; the standalone "Line" name is dropped per the verifier.
- BEST AGAINST: man; a common counter-shape starts the stack on the lane line OPPOSITE the ball (Coach's Clipboard "Stack-6").
- URLS (opened): playbook PDF pg. 16-18 and 52-53 · https://www.coachesclipboard.net/OBStackPlays.html · https://hoopmentality.com/blogs/basketball/baseline-out-of-bounds-plays-guide-for-coaches · name second-sourced https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-run-a-stack-play-in-basketball

3. 4-LOW / 4-ACROSS (BLOB) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: parks all four teammates at or below the block line, emptying everything above the FT line, so one baseline runner off staggered screens, or one popper into the vacuum, gets a clean catch. Also the standard safe shape when the ball simply must come in.
- FULL PACKAGE (Preheim "4 Low Pop", pg. 39): inbounder 3, baseline ballside. Alignment four across the baseline: 2 weakside corner, 4 weakside block, 5 ballside block, 1 ballside corner. Action: 2 runs the baseline off staggered screens from 4 then 5, catches in the BALLSIDE CORNER for the catch-and-shoot; 1 clears to the top as safety. Receiver's defender: starts on 2 in the weakside corner, chases the baseline through two screens, trails the catch by a body. Counter (pg. 41): if 2 has no shot, 3 cuts behind 2 for a handoff three. Variant (pg. 38): 4 inbounds, 5 and 2 pop to the FT-line area, 5 gets the pass into flex action; Hoops Geek "4 Low Clear": 1 inbounds, 4 pops beyond the elbow, lob/iso.
- BEST AGAINST: man; also the pressure-relief shape of choice (nothing to deny below the rim line).
- URLS: playbook PDF pg. 38-41 (opened) · https://www.thehoopsgeek.com/basketball-inbound-plays/ (opened) · https://www.coachesclipboard.net/OB4LowPlays.html (name-confirming, surfaced in search, NOT opened; do not lean on its contents).

4. AMERICA'S PLAY (BLOB, screen-the-inbounder) · CONFIRMED name with an AMBIGUITY FLAG
- PROBLEM: defenses habitually ignore the inbounder after the pass; this set makes the inbounder the shooter, screening them as they step back in.
- FULL PACKAGE (Hoops Geek play library, opened): inbounder 1, your shooter, baseline ballside. On the signal: 4 pops to the ballside corner from the ballside block, 3 pops to the ballside wing, 2 pops to the weakside wing, 5 holds at the weakside block/under the rim. First receiver: 4 in the ballside corner, deliberately the conceded pass; his defender sagging toward the lane. Then 4 swings to 3 and screens for 1 stepping in; 1 fakes using 5's screen, uses 4's, and 3 hits 1 for the shot. Safety valve: 5 or 4 under the basket.
- AMBIGUITY (from the name-reality lens, opened): Coach's Clipboard's "America's Play" is a different HALFCOURT staggered down-screen set, and FastModel says outright there was not a singular America's Play. If it ships, the game owns one meaning and should expect coaches to know the other.
- BEST AGAINST: man; the whole mechanism is the inbounder's defender relaxing.
- URLS (opened): https://www.thehoopsgeek.com/basketball-plays/america-s-play-blob/ · https://www.coachesclipboard.net/AmericaPlay.html (the other meaning)

5. ZIPPER (SLOB entry) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: pressure defenses deny the point guard the inbound catch; the zipper screen delivers the PG the ball at the top with their defender trailing, flowing straight into a ballscreen. The cleanest "get organized" entry, which is why NBA teams script it out of timeouts.
- FULL PACKAGE: inbounder 3, SIDELINE, at/just above FT-line extended (consistent across every Preheim Sideout diagram, pg. 116-124). Alignment: box or "pro set" (box with a corner man instead of a block): 1 ballside block, 4 ballside elbow stacked above 1 on the lane line, 5 weakside elbow/top, 2 weakside corner. Action, verbatim from the opened page: 4 screens, 1 zipper cuts, 3 passes to 1; catch at the TOP OF THE KEY; 4 relocates to the corner; 5 times a ballscreen arrival so 1 drives, 5 rolls, 1 reads. Receiver's defender: denying 1 low at the block, forced to trail the vertical cut, arriving behind the play. Caveat kept: the per-player spot list is a faithful reading of the pro-set diagram, not quoted text (THIN on exact spots, uncontradicted). Kosel's collection shows Golden State, Oregon, Mexico, Croatia, LSU versions.
- BEST AGAINST: denial/pressure man.
- URLS (opened): https://www.thehoopsgeek.com/basketball-plays/zipper-slob/ · https://www.mensbasketballhoopscoop.com/slob-zipper-plays-wes-kosel/ . Golden State "Zip Quick" and the .973 PPP SLOB figure: SNIPPET-ONLY (source 403).

6. ELEVATOR (SLOB) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: a chasing defender cannot go over, under, or around two screeners shoulder to shoulder; closing the gap behind the shooter erases the trail defender for one catch-and-shoot beat.
- FULL PACKAGE, three documented forms:
  (a) SLOB form (Drills&Plays, opened): inbounder on the sideline at FT-line extended; entry to 4 stepping to the slot; two screeners form a double stack in the upper lane/top of the key with a one-body gap; the shooter starts LOW at the ballside block, sprints up the middle of the lane through the gap, the doors close, catch at the top for three.
  (b) Pinned BLOB form with exact spots (Hoop Coach, opened, verbatim): inbounder 3 baseline; 5 on a block, 2 on the opposite block, 4 on the elbow on 5's side, 1 at the top of the key; 2 cuts between 4 and 5 as the elevator and catches the inbound; counter is a swing to 1 and a pin-down for the inbounder.
  (c) Direct-inbound variant (Preheim pg. 16): four in a line across the FT-line area (4-2-1-5), inbounder 3 baseline ballside; 1 curls for the inbound, 4 and 5 close the gap, 2 steps back for the three.
- Receiver's defender: chasing from the block, expected stuck in the doors below the catch point.
- BEST FOR: manufacturing one clean three for a designated shooter vs man; signature NBA use is Golden State freeing Curry/Thompson (league film writeup, opened).
- URLS (opened): https://www.drillsandplays.com/en/playbooks/1747-slob-s-elevator · https://www.hoopcoach.org/elevator-door-screen-and-pin-down-screen-baseline-out-of-bounds-play/ · https://www.nbcsports.com/nba/news/warriors-use-elevator-screens-to-perfection-to-free-up-their-shooters-video · https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/elevator-screen-basketball/ · playbook PDF pg. 16

Q2. DEFENSIVE PACKAGES
=====================================================
All from opened pages; two dead URLs in the ledger. Naming caveats from the adversarial pass are inline.

1. MAN-TO-MAN (base halfcourt shell) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: gives every offensive player a personal defender so no one is ever uncontested, with help built in behind the ball.
- FULL PACKAGE: on-ball defender picks up at the arc, arm's length, steering sideline/baseline, never middle. One pass away (ball-side wing and corner): denial, hand and foot in the lane. Two passes away: sunk to the lane line in ball-you-man help stance. Ball on the wing: on-ball overplays middle, corner in full denial, weak side at the lane line. Ball in the corner: on-ball walls the baseline, helpers stack high-I (elbow) and low-I (mid-lane). Pickup point is a dial: quarter, half, three-quarter, or full-court pressure man.
- PUNISHES: passive, screen-light offenses; weak handlers.
- BEATEN BY: PnR and off-ball screens (switch confusion, mismatches); cannot hide a weak defender; screening BLOBs target it.
- URLS (opened): https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/man-to-man-defense/ · https://www.breakthroughbasketball.com/defense/pressure

2. PACK LINE (sagging man, Dick Bennett family) · CONFIRMED (verifier spot-checked)
- PROBLEM: kills dribble penetration by keeping four bodies packed inside an imaginary line 16 feet from the rim.
- FULL PACKAGE: one defender pressures the ball hard; the other four stand INSIDE the pack line in gap positions regardless of their men: ball-side gap defender at the ball-side elbow/slot, weak-side wing defender at the weak elbow, corner men shaded from mid-lane, big at the ball-side block. No denial one pass away; closeouts on the catch. Exception, verbatim-confirmed: on a dead dribble all five jump to full denial. Minor gap the verifier noted: the page does not pin the pickup point to the arc specifically.
- PUNISHES: drive-heavy guards; teams without shooting.
- BEATEN BY: three-point shooting (the page's lead weakness) and quick swings forcing long closeouts.
- URLS (opened): https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/pack-line-defense/ · pedigree https://grantland.com/the-triangle/virginia-defense-pack-line-dick-tony-bennett/

3. 2-3 ZONE (Syracuse/Boeheim) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: protects the paint and the rebound while hiding weak or foul-prone defenders, because each man guards ground.
- FULL PACKAGE: two guards at the top of the key splitting the slots, nearly touching hands; two forwards a step outside each block owning wings and corners below the FT line; center in the middle of the key. Shift on the flight of the ball; guards pinch middle. Pickup: top of the key, one defender on the ball at a time.
- PUNISHES: impatient teams, teams that never practice zone offense, foul-drawing drivers.
- BEATEN BY: outside shooting, patient swings, and a high-post catch at the FT line.
- LEAGUE REALISM (confirmed, with window caveat): NBA zone on only 2-4 percent of possessions, about 0.05 PPP worse than man, Charlotte's 13.6 percent the peak; data window 2019-2022, so treat as a change-up in NBA fiction. WNBA: Golden State ran a consistent 2-3 all season (confirmed).
- URLS (opened): https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/2-3-zone-defense/ · https://www.basketballpoetry.com/p/the-twilight-zone-how-much-zone-defense · https://jsleeprstats.substack.com/p/wnba-defensive-scheme-and-tendency · name pedigree https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2%E2%80%933_zone_defense

4. 3-2 ZONE · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: puts three defenders across the arc to smother perimeter passing and wing shooters while two bigs hold the paint.
- FULL PACKAGE: rover at the FT line (no pressure on the point; denies the high post, picks off lobs out of traps); two wings at FT-line extended each side (trap corners, defend wings, help in the key); two tallest players on the blocks (challenge the key, cover corners, rebound). Pickup effectively at the wings, since the top gets no ball pressure.
- PUNISHES: perimeter-oriented offenses; takes away wing catch-and-shoots.
- BEATEN BY: high-post play, corner shooters, penetration through the unpressured top.
- URL (opened): https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/3-2-zone-defense/

5. 1-3-1 ZONE (half-court trap) · CONFIRMED (verifier spot-checked), one jargon note
- PROBLEM: turns a zone into a turnover machine by trapping the corners and denying every reversal.
- FULL PACKAGE: chaser at the FT line pushing to the top of the key (pickup point; forces one side, denies the guard-to-guard reversal); two wings just outside the arc at FT-line extended, one up ball-side, one down weak-side covering the weak block; center fronting the post at all times; baseline runner sprinting corner to corner, fronting the ball-side block. Hard traps in baseline corners, soft traps in half-court corners; the sideline pass down must be denied or lobbed.
- PUNISHES: the "teams that panic against pressure" line is UNVERIFIED (not on the opened page; needs a second source). What IS sourced: it feeds fast breaks off deflections.
- BEATEN BY (verbatim-confirmed): the corners and the high post; skip passes to the weak wing then corner.
- JARGON NOTE: "chaser/warrior" role names are Basketball For Coaches house vocabulary, not league-wide (elsewhere: "baseline runner"/"tail"); the scheme name itself is universal.
- URL (opened): https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/1-3-1-zone-defense/

6. MATCHUP ZONE · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: makes the offense unable to tell man from zone, so neither of its offenses fits.
- FULL PACKAGE: shown from a 2-1-2 base: two guards in the slots, center mid-key, two posts on the blocks (also shown 1-3-1 or 1-2-2). Rules on top: tight pressure on the ball wherever it crosses the arc (pickup: the arc); free switching keeps bigs low and guards outside; low post three-quarter or full fronted; wings deny entries; man-style help on penetration; ball-side cuts chased man-to-man.
- PUNISHES: scouted teams; the base look baits the wrong offense.
- BEATEN BY: offenses with rules for it: overloads and ball-side cutting stress the switch rules.
- URLS (opened): https://www.coachesclipboard.net/MatchUpZone.html · pedigree https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match-up_zone_defense

7. BOX-AND-ONE (junk) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: removes one dominant scorer from the game entirely while four zone the paint.
- FULL PACKAGE: chaser (quickest) face-guards the star in full denial everywhere, never helping (pickup: wherever the star is); two high box defenders at the elbows covering perimeter and high post; two low box defenders (tallest) at the blocks covering paint and glass. On the star's catch, all five collapse, then re-form.
- PUNISHES: one-star offenses; exhausts the star through denial.
- BEATEN BY: role players making shots, perimeter teams (only two defenders reach the arc, none the corners), skips, and post scorers congesting the box's own paint.
- URLS (opened): https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/box-and-1-defense/ · pedigree (2019 Finals, Nurse/VanVleet on Curry) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box-and-one_defense

8. TRIANGLE-AND-TWO (junk) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: erases TWO stars at once.
- FULL PACKAGE: two chasers in full denial on the two best scorers, never helping or rotating; top triangle defender at the top of the key owning high post and ball above the FT line; two bottom triangle defenders (bigs) on the blocks, fronting the low post when the ball is high, taking the ball man below, giving weak-side help. Pickup: FT line for the triangle, wherever their men are for the chasers.
- PUNISHES: two-man offenses; the other three must beat you.
- BEATEN BY: middle penetration, third and fourth shooters, block scorers, and offensive rebounding (a three-man triangle boxes out badly).
- URLS (opened): https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/triangle-and-2-defense/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle-and-two_defense

9. 1-2-1-1 DIAMOND PRESS (full-court) · CONFIRMED, jargon note
- PROBLEM: attacks the inbound pass itself, forcing the ball to a corner and trapping the first catch before the offense organizes. The one defense whose spec literally starts at the opponent's inbound, so it maps onto the game's dead-ball moment with zero translation.
- FULL PACKAGE: disruptor on the inbounder at the baseline, waving the pass toward a coffin corner; two wings a few steps behind the nearest receivers on each side, inviting the corner catch; interceptor in line with the inbounder, halfway between the offensive arc and half-court; safety as deep as the deepest player, guarding the 2-on-1. On the corner catch: near wing plus disruptor trap (wing seals sideline, disruptor takes the middle pass, high hands); interceptor reads the outlet. Pickup: the inbound pass, full court. "Disruptor" is BFC house vocabulary; the "diamond press" name is cross-source.
- PUNISHES: tempo-hating teams, weak inbounders, one-handler rosters.
- BEATEN BY: a pass to the middle (its stated primary weakness), sideline dribble splits, accurate skips; one blown assignment opens everything.
- URLS (opened): https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/1-2-1-1-diamond-press/ · name cross-source https://www.thehoopsgeek.com/full-court-press-defense/

10. 2-2-1 PRESS (three-quarter court) · CONFIRMED
- PROBLEM: slows the advance and burns clock without gambling on the inbound; herds the ball sideline and traps before half-court. Strategic contrast the picker can feel: this press deliberately ALLOWS the inbound pass, the diamond attacks it.
- FULL PACKAGE: two guards at the elbows of the offense's backcourt FT line, hands high, engaging only after the catch and forcing sideline; two bigs a step or two inside half-court closing the sideline lane and springing the trap; best interceptor deep around the defensive arc reading everything. Traps on the sideline between the backcourt FT line and half-court. Pickup: after the inbound completes, roughly three-quarter court. Personnel variance (HoopsKing assigns forwards to the middle line): SNIPPET-ONLY.
- PUNISHES: teams without strong handlers or over-the-top passing; backcourt-violation generator.
- BEATEN BY: cuts and passes through the middle, lobs over the sideline traps.
- URL (opened): https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/2-2-1-press/

PICK-AND-ROLL COVERAGE DIAL (calls layered on the Man pick, not standalone setups; all names CONFIRMED, aliases independently verified)
- DROP: screener's defender sags to the paint, on-ball chases over. Stops rim pressure and the roll; beaten by pull-up shooters, stack/veer screens, re-screens. WNBA anchor (confirmed): Atlanta plays drop; Indiana's ice/drop identity struggles vs perimeter guards.
- ICE (also "Down"/"Blue"; aliases confirmed): on-ball jumps high side and refuses the screen, forcing sideline; big shows hips to the sideline. Kills middle use of side screens; beaten by rejecting the screen, re-screens to the middle, snaking.
- HEDGE (show and recover): screener's defender jumps out hard then sprints back. Beaten by slow recovery handing the roller the rim.
- BLITZ: both defenders trap above the screen. Stops elite pull-up shooters, forces turnovers (18-22 percent vs unprepared offenses per the source); beaten by the early short-roll pass into 4-on-3, kick-and-relocate, weak-corner skips.
- SWITCH EVERYTHING: trade assignments at contact; needs five switchable bodies. Kills the two-man game itself; beaten by hunting the worst defender, ghost screens, staggers forcing a second switch. WNBA anchors (all six quotes confirmed on the opened page): Las Vegas "switching everything beyond what anyone else is doing in the league"; New York switch-first with Stewart roaming; Minnesota switch-heavy with a ghost-switch wrinkle; the Aces' listed vulnerability is guards switched onto big forwards.
- URLS (opened): https://hoopbrief.com/blog/pick-and-roll-coverages-explained-drop-switch-blitz-ice · https://jsleeprstats.substack.com/p/wnba-defensive-scheme-and-tendency · alias source https://betterbasketball.com/nba-terminology-iceblueweak/ · https://www.basketballforcoaches.com/hedge-basketball/

SHORTLIST: THE TWO PICKABLE LISTS
=====================================================
OFFENSE SETUPS (8). Chosen for distinct grid silhouettes, real vocabulary, dead-ball fit, and each being best against a different defensive shape.
1. HORNS: the all-purpose modern set; elbows full, paint empty, reads instantly on a grid, and one shape carries many outcomes (Twist/Flare/Chase), the purest match for "starting shapes only".
2. FIVE-OUT (DELAY): all five on the arc, the anti-zone anti-drop spacing pick, and the visual opposite of everything else on the list.
3. FLOPPY: shooter starts UNDER THE RIM, a silhouette no other set has, and the pick that punishes man denial with a catch-and-shoot.
4. SPAIN: the stacked middle lane reads as its own shape and it is the pick that beats drop coverage specifically.
5. IVERSON (1-4 HIGH): four across the FT line flattens the defense, the pick against star denial, with backdoors against overplay.
6. BOX (BLOB): the canonical baseline inbound shape, four in a square inside the lane, works vs man AND zone, so it is the safe default pick.
7. 4-LOW (BLOB): four flat on the baseline, the vacuum-above shape, and the pressure-relief pick when the inbound itself is threatened.
8. ZIPPER (SLOB): the sideline dead-ball organizer, PG freed to the top into instant PnR, the pick against pressure man.
Bench: Elevator (shot-manufacturing SLOB, more scripted intent than a pure shape), Stack (silhouette overlaps Zipper's lane-line file), Chin (great vs pressure but single-sourced package), Pistol (real name, but explicitly early offense, no dead-ball form), Flex and Iso and Triangle (era packs), America's Play (name ambiguity on record).

DEFENSE SETUPS (6). Ordered casual to nerdy; each answers a different offensive shape.
1. MAN-TO-MAN: the base pick; individual pressure everywhere, and the shape all four junk/zone picks are defined against.
2. PACK LINE: the anti-drive pick; four bodies inside 16 feet, concedes the arc, the direct counter to Iso and rim attacks.
3. 2-3 ZONE: the paint-packing pick that punishes impatience; reads instantly (2 up top, 3 across) and carries the NBA change-up / WNBA Golden State identity.
4. 1-3-1 TRAP: the turnover-hunting half-court pick with named soft spots (corners, high post) that a smart offense pick can target.
5. BOX-AND-ONE: the star-eraser; one chaser face-guards, four zone, the pick that makes the opponent's role players answer trivia.
6. DIAMOND PRESS (1-2-1-1): the only defense specced FROM the inbound itself, so it is the pick that attacks the dead-ball moment the game is built on; its foil, the 2-2-1 (concedes the inbound), is first off the bench.
Second dial, not list entries: Drop / Ice / Hedge / Blitz / Switch layered on the Man pick, since they only describe the two defenders at a screen; WNBA team identities (Vegas switch, Atlanta drop, Indiana ice, NY switch-roam) support flavoring opponents by coverage identity.
