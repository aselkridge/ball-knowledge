Ball Knowledge · a finding, and a choice for you

The courts you already own

"why was the floor just colored panels if I had went and sourced the floors all that time ago?"Aaron, 08-19

Because the game’s default court loads none of it. Five court families are sitting in the repo, 4.9MB of arena backdrops and floor textures you paid for, and the default (Classic) hits one line that says: use no art. Every game you have played, and every screenshot I have sent you, has been the hand-coded placeholder floor.

Finding one. applyCourt() begins if the court is classic, use no art and return. The default is classic-a. So the sourced hardwood, blacktop, cosmic and underwater floors, plus ten arena backdrops, only appear if a player goes into the court picker and chooses one.

Finding two, and it explains why hardwood and blacktop look almost alike below. When art IS on, the game paints a checkerboard over it at 16 percent to keep the tiles readable. That much checker mutes the texture underneath, so an expensive floor arrives looking like a dark grid. The first panel shows the same hardwood with the checker at 5 percent: same art, one number, and the wood finally breathes.

And my part in it. Last night I hand-drew plank lines onto the placeholder floor to make it feel like wood, while your photographed wood sat unused one config line away. My own instructions say to check whether it already exists before drawing anything. I did not check.

The choice: which court is the game’s default?

What I would do

  • Make Hardwood the default, with the checker dialed back so your texture is visible. Classic stays in the picker for anyone who wants the clean look.
  • Retune the checker for every art court, not just hardwood: the tiles stay legible on their grid lines and the move-range tints, which are already doing most of the work.
  • Then judge the board overhaul again. The grounding work (apron, contact shadows, jerseyed numbers) is real, but I was polishing the placeholder. On the actual floor it reads differently, and some of it may turn out unnecessary.
