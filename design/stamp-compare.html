What you gain and give up

Option A

Heavier, louder, rougher

  • GAINThick strokes survive a moving court and a small screen — it still reads at phone size.
  • GAINThe flames are structural, so it looks like fire even at a glance.
  • COSTBusier. Over a crowded possession it competes with the pieces underneath.
  • COSTSquare, so it eats more vertical space on a phone.
Option B

Cleaner, faster, more premium

  • GAINBetter lettering. The brush strokes have real hand-drawn confidence.
  • GAINWide format sits naturally across a landscape court.
  • COSTThin strokes fight the court behind them, especially on a phone.
  • COSTReads more as a logo than as an impact — less like a hit, more like a title.
My call, and why

A — but it is close, and B is not wrong

This stamp exists for two seconds, over a moving court, mostly on a phone. That is an impact frame, not a logo lockup, and impact frames want weight. A survives the phone test above; B starts to thin out against the floor.

The honest counter: B is the better piece of lettering. If this were going on a title screen, a shirt, or the app icon, I would pick B without hesitating. If you love B, take it — the cost is legibility on small screens, and it is a real cost, not a fatal one.

There is a third option: use both. A slams during play; B becomes the wordmark for the Daily Five receipt, the rulebook heat section, and anywhere the word sits still. They are the same idea at two volumes, and we already own both.
