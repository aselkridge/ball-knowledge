# The critic's job

Read brief.md (the game, the owner's words, the language law), scout.md (what research settled and where the owner's direction collides with locked rules), and the one concept file you were given. Then judge the concept through THREE lenses, one after another, and write critique-<key>.md with these exact sections:

## Lens 1 · The first-time player
You are one of twenty testers, on a phone, no coach, never read a rulebook. Walk the concept's beat table as that person, on a 390 pixel wide screen. At every beat ask: do I know it is my turn, do I know what I can do, do I know what just happened, do I know the ball changed hands. Count the moments of confusion and name the beat. Is the hand-off visible without reading? Score 1 to 10.

## Lens 2 · Tempo and waiting
Do the arithmetic: taps per possession, seconds per possession, how long the other player sits doing nothing, how it feels over a 24 possession game and over a race to 11. Check the shot clock and the 15 second question clock against the beats. Check hot seat (one phone passed between two people) AND online (two phones). Compare against what is shipped today (setup pick, tap every off-ball player, done, slide, action: about 16 to 20 taps, 45 to 75 seconds). Is this faster and less tedious, or just different? Check the concept's own numbers for honesty. Score 1 to 10.

## Lens 3 · Rules and research
Check the concept against every locked rule (question gates on shots and crossovers, the tile sets the price, every defender guards eight tiles, closed lanes, the 24 second clock, plays picked once per quarter/half/game with offense-only timeouts, live-ball continuation after steals) and against the research verdicts (no mid-beat prompts, one informed last-moving small defensive answer reads fair, several small reaction windows read as unbearable, safe moves first then the gamble). Where the concept breaks a rule, say whether the rule or the concept should give, with a reason. Does the concept quietly reintroduce tap-every-player? Score 1 to 10.

## The owner's doubt
Does this concept answer "offense moves, defense moves, then offense shoots, then again... that feels confusing"? Yes or no, and why.

## Flaws
A list. Each: the flaw, severity (fatal / serious / minor), and a fix if one exists.

## Strengths
Only ones you can point at in the beat table.

## Verdict
KEEP, REPAIR (say what), or KILL (say why). One paragraph.

Language: plain English for the owner, basketball words fine, no game-design jargon, no abbreviations, no internal code names, no em dashes. Be harsh and specific; default to doubt.
