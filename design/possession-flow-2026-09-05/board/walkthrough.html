0

The words this page uses

A possession
The stretch from when your team gets the ball to when the other team gets it.
A turn
One side's go. You take a turn, then they take a turn, back and forth until the ball changes teams.
A question
The trivia card that fills the screen. Four answers, fifteen seconds. Shooting always brings one up. Dribbling into a square a defender is guarding brings one up (that is a crossover). A long pass or a pass through traffic brings one up for the passer. Get it right and the thing you tried works. Get it wrong and it fails.
The balls
The little row of balls above your buttons. Each ball is one of your turns. When the last ball is gone you must have shot, or the ball goes to the other team. This is the shot clock of this game, counted in turns instead of seconds. The 24-second clock is a different thing: it times one turn, so nobody can sit and think forever. The balls time the whole possession.
A free move
Once a turn, before you do anything with the ball, you may move one teammate who does not have the ball. It costs nothing and you can skip it.
The step
The defense's turn: move one defender. That is the whole turn, unless you go for a steal instead.
A steal
A defender who is already standing next to the ball handler when the defensive turn starts can try to take the ball. It is one question for the defender. Right, and he has the ball. Wrong, and the ball handler gets one free square past him.
A blow-by
You dribbled into a defender's square, answered your question right, and he failed to stay in front of you. You are past him. When that happens you get one more thing straight away, a shot or a pass, before the defense gets its step. The screen says ONE MORE.
A lane
The straight line a pass would travel. A defender standing on that line makes the pass cost the passer a question. Nobody "picks" a lane in secret; you guard a lane by standing in it, and the screen shows you which lanes your defender is cutting while you hold him.
A live ball
The ball changes teams without a stop: a steal, or a rebound. Everyone stays where they are and the new offense has to bring the ball up the floor.
The glide
After a made basket, both teams slide into their chosen plays at the other end of the floor automatically, with the ball already in the point guard's hands. No inbound pass to tap, no walking it up.
A violation
The whistle. Either you did not get the ball over half court in time, or you did not shoot in time. The other team gets the ball.

The rules, all of them, on one screen

This is the whole rulebook as recommended. Eleven sentences. Everything below this section is these sentences played out.

  1. Your turn: one free move of a teammate without the ball (you can skip it), then one thing with the ball: dribble, pass or shoot. That one thing ends your turn.
  2. Their turn: one defender moves. Or, if a defender is already next to the ball, he can go for the steal instead. Never both.
  3. A crossover: dribbling into a guarded square brings up a question. Answer right and the defender gets a question to stay in front of you. If he fails, you are past him and you get ONE MORE: a shot or a pass, right away, no defensive step in between. Once a turn. The man you beat cannot contest that shot. Anyone else standing next to you and between you and the rim still can.
  4. A missed steal gives the ball handler one free square past the reacher.
  5. The balls: after a made basket you have three turns to shoot. On a live ball you have five, and the ball must be over half court by the end of your second turn. Miss either and it is a violation.
  6. The clocks: the 24-second clock times each offensive turn, as today. The defense gets 10 seconds for its step. Let either run out and you lose that turn.
  7. Lanes: a defender guards a pass by standing in its path. While you hold a defender on your turn, the squares where he would cut a pass are tinted. There is no secret lane pick.
  8. After a made basket both teams glide into their plays at the other end. After a steal or a rebound, play goes on from where everyone stands.
  9. Picking your plays: each team picks its offense the first time it has the ball and its defense the first time it does not. The defense picks first and the offense sees it. A timeout, which only the offense can call, brings the pick screen back.
  10. Bookkeeping: an offensive rebound gives two more turns. A timeout changes nothing on the balls. Over-and-back, three seconds in the key and out of bounds work as they do today, with the key counted in turns.
  11. The big man may camp in the paint. There is no defensive three-second rule. Beat your man and you may still meet him.

What I need from you: twelve decisions

Each one has my recommendation in green and the other options under it. Answer with the number and a word. "1 yes. 2 four. 3 no, play the walk-up." That is enough.

1. The balls: how many turns, and where the half-court line falls.
Recommended: three turns to shoot after a made basket. On a live ball, five turns, with the ball over half court by the end of the second. One count, so crossing early never earns you extra turns.
Other options: your original (three before half court, then three more after, which rewards holding the ball back); or three to cross and six in all. The paper test can settle the exact number if you would rather not pick it now.
Your answer: yes, or your numbers
2. The ball handler's turn ends on a dribble, a pass or a shot.
Recommended: yes. A plain dribble to an open square uses the turn the same as a pass does. Your list said pass, crossover or shot; the plain dribble was missing, and without it the ball handler could walk the floor for free.
Your answer: yes or no
3. The free move can be skipped at no cost.
Recommended: yes. Tapping the ball handler, a pass ring or Shoot skips it in zero taps. If it were a required step, every possession would carry four extra taps that nobody wants, which is the thing you complained about in the first place.
Your answer: yes or no
4. The defense does one thing a turn.
Recommended: one defender steps, or a defender already next to the ball goes for the steal. Never both. With a move and a steal every turn, the numbers say the defense takes the ball about a third of the time per try, and over a possession the offense keeps it about one time in five. That is the "you cannot dribble or pass without getting robbed" complaint from the closest game to ours.
Other option: a move and then an action, as you floated. If you want that, it needs a limit on how often the steal can be tried.
Your answer: one thing, or move then action
5. The steal becomes one question.
Recommended: the defender answers one question. Right, he has the ball. Wrong, the ball handler gets one free square past him (your steal-costing ruling). Today a steal is two questions and a tap race, about half a minute; one question halves that.
Your answer: yes or keep today's two questions and the tap race
6. No secret lane pick. The step is the lane pick.
Recommended: a defender guards a pass by standing in its path, and while you hold him the lanes he would cut are tinted. The secret version (a special player names a lane in private) stays a skill some players get later, for online play only, because on one shared phone nothing is secret.
Other option: build the secret lane pick into the base game now.
Your answer: yes, or build it now
7. ONE MORE is a shot or a pass, your choice, and the man you beat cannot contest.
Recommended: yes. Read literally, "the shot in range, a free pass out of range" deletes drive-and-kick, the most basic play in basketball. The pass is free of the turn, not free of its question if it is long or through traffic. And the shot contest today has no memory of who was just beaten, so the rule has to say the beaten man is out.
Your answer: yes or no
8. After a made basket, both teams glide into their plays.
Recommended: yes. Playing the walk-up turn by turn after every made basket costs about 38 seconds and five turn changes, three of them a defensive turn where the honest move is to stay put. Over a game that is 7 to 9 minutes of nothing. The half-court count still bites where it matters, on steals and rebounds.
Other option: play the walk-up every time.
Your answer: glide, or play the walk-up
9. The defense's step gets a 10-second clock.
Recommended: 10 seconds, and running out forfeits the step. Today the defense has its own 24 (your July ruling, "both sides get to think"). Under a turn count, a 24-second defensive clock lets the defense stall for two and a half minutes a possession with nothing lost. The balls do not fix that; only a short step does.
Other options: 8 seconds; or keep the 24.
Your answer: 10, 8, or keep 24
10. At each first pick, the defense picks first and the offense sees it.
Recommended: yes, at both moments: after the tip, and at the first change of possession. This is the research's answer to your blind-or-sighted question: whoever commits blind feels cheated, so the defense commits first and gets to set the terms.
Other options: both pick blind; or the offense picks first.
Your answer: defense first, blind, or offense first
11. The big man may camp in the paint.
Recommended: yes, say so in the rulebook. The strategy you want people to build around (beat your man, meet the big) is legal in college and international ball and illegal in the NBA. Ruling it legal here keeps your idea.
Your answer: yes or no
12. The small bookkeeping, as a set.
Recommended: an offensive rebound gives two more turns. A timeout changes nothing on the balls (or timeouts become the new way to stall). A steal or a rebound starts the five-ball count from where the ball lies. The balls are called turns and the 24 keeps its name, so "shot clock" means one thing. Three seconds in the key counts turns.
Your answer: yes, or name the one you want changed

When these twelve are answered, the next step is a paper test: a printed court, two people, one afternoon, playing exactly this, counting seconds and turn changes. Then a clickable mock-up on the real court. Then the game. Nothing gets built before the paper test.
