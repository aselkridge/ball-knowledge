## 1. What is already answered

### (a) The play menu

**Verdict on file: defense picks from 3, offense from 4 to 5, hard ceiling 6, everything visible at once, one tap, no scrolling, no two-step trees, the defense's pick shown to the offense on purpose.** Source: design/22ai-findings.md, "Run B's sizing, defended", and the raw return design/archive/22ai-runB-return-2026-08-13.md, "Recommendation: the pick-flow". Source games: Slay the Spire, Hades and Baldur's Gate 3 for the 3-choice standard; Clash Royale's four-card hand and Tecmo Bowl's four plays for the offense count; Teamfight Tactics' five as the shipped ceiling; NBA 2K25 for "a curated short set beats a deep library" and "never a two-hop tree on a phone"; College Football 25 for the one-default-plus-three-alternates shape; Retro Bowl for "the most successful phone football game pushed the pick toward zero"; Hearthstone Battlegrounds for the fact that timer complaints run in both directions.

**The lists themselves are chosen.** Shortlist of 8 offensive and 6 defensive sets in design/22ai-findings.md, "The two shortlists". Aaron picked the universal three each side on 08-16 ("nahh I liked your recs"): MAN, 2-3 ZONE, BOX-AND-ONE; HORNS, FIVE-OUT, FLOPPY. Recorded in V0.md, the "FULL SETUP IS RULED" block, "STEP 2 IS ANSWERED". The Setup Book artifact draws every shortlist set on the game's court. The three "Up the Floor" proposals (two-part advance after a made basket, set-back-or-press, always-three-universal menus) are drawn but not ruled.

**What loosened on 09-05:** TODO.md row 238 records Aaron's second message: the pick happens once per quarter, half or game, "so I don't mind all of them being listed because it's not that often, they can take up most of the screen". The row itself says the 3-and-4 sizing "was for an every-possession pick and no longer binds". The all-visible, one-tap, no-tree rules still hold; the count does not.

**Blind or sighted, who picks first (his open question in row 238) is answered on file, twice.** design/d37-defense-findings.md, Q4 "The telegraph shape": Tecmo Bowl's hidden simultaneous pick is REJECTED ("basically a game of Rock, Paper, Scissors"; a visible call converts the coin flip into a read); Slay the Spire's two rules ADOPTED (the telegraph is honest and coarse, never lies, never tells everything); Hoplite ADOPTED for delivery (the call painted on the board, never in a modal); Sirlin's Yomi test (every call must be best against a different offensive shape, or cut one). design/22ai-findings.md repeats it: "graded payoffs balanced for perfect information (the defense's pick is shown on purpose)". And DESIGN.md section 3 has it as a built rule: "the defense picks and reveals its setup FIRST and the offense picks seeing it." The research says: defense first, visible.

### (b) The defensive answer

**Verdict on file: THE CALL AND THE SLIDE.** design/d37-defense-findings.md, "The recommendation, revised". One visible coverage call at the handoff before the offense's setup, then one defender making one small capped move AFTER the offense's action, with full information, and the missed question as the equalizer. On Aaron's desk since 08-12; never ruled. His own 08-12 theory (V0.md, same block, "AARON'S OWN THEORY") converged on it from the basketball side and re-timed the slide to fall after the setup and before the action; that re-timing is what shipped as Method B.

The named verdicts, each with its game (all in design/d37-defense-findings.md):
- Kill Team's "Counteract" (ADOPT, the closest shipped match, spot-checked accurate): the out-of-actions side gets one free capped move between each enemy action, resolving after the enemy action with full information. "It reads as fair not because it matches volume but because it moves LAST with full information."
- Into the Breach (ADOPT the principle): few, small, fully informed moves against a revealed threat; under a 15-second beat "the informed reaction needs a SMALL legal-option set; one defender, 1-2 squares, is right-sized."
- Blood Bowl (ADAPT the risk half, corrected): the defense gets no activations during the offense's turn; the real counterweight is the turnover rule, a failed offensive action ends the turn.
- DreadBall (REJECT the timing, ADOPT the sizing): one interrupt, one-square cap, big payoff, but it fires mid-turn, "the shape mobile cannot carry".
- Wargroove (REJECT mirrored full-team movement): "I want slick, clean, fast, responsive game, where I can focus on strategy and not on waiting."
- Hero Academy and Magic Arena (REJECT several small reaction windows): "unbearable... so slow"; interleaving a micro-reaction after each step rebuilds priority-passing inside a shot clock. Both flagged snippet-level sources.
- XCOM overwatch (REJECT blind pre-commitment): must be penalized or it dominates; a blind commitment cannot answer a setup it never saw.
- Basketball Strategy, Avalon Hill 1974 (ADAPT): no defender moves at all; one call per possession against a results matrix.
- Statis Pro Basketball (ADAPT the double-team): "send help" as one declared decision with an automatic cost elsewhere.
- Hoop League Tactics (ADAPT with caveats, "the closest cousin"): defense is a standing scheme, "you watch the action unfold", and the verified App Store complaint is "the defending system. It's an absolute nightmare." Single-player only.
- Football, Tactics and Glory (the warning): defense fires automatically by position during the attacker's block; balance record "more reliance on luck than strategy". Zero-tap auto-defense is hard to tune and opaque.
- Blood Bowl 3 (the corrected finding): the one digital sports game that prompts the non-active player mid-turn, "and that is exactly where it bleeds". Hence the standing rule: no mid-beat prompts to the defender, ever.

**One finding in that file speaks directly to the 09-05 direction and was set aside for a reason that no longer holds.** design/d37-defense-findings.md, Q1, Guild Ball: "Every game that achieved true move-for-move parity did it by abolishing the team turn (strict alternating single-piece activations)." The raw return (design/archive/d37-workflow-return-2026-08-12.md, Findings 1) gives the reason it was rejected: "REJECT as structure (alternating possessions are locked, and per-piece alternation multiplies taps per beat)." Strict alternating single-piece activation is exactly what Aaron described on 09-05. The research never weighed it on its merits; the brief forbade it (design/d37-defense-brief.md: "do NOT propose simultaneous turns or full team-turns"), and its one stated cost, taps per beat, is the very complaint he now has against the shipped setup half. Kill Team, the run's own ADOPT, is also an alternating one-unit-at-a-time game; only its Counteract rule was taken.

### (c) The order of safe moves and gambles

**Verdict on file: safe moves first, the gamble last, a failed gamble ends the whole turn (ADAPT, the strongest-verified finding of the first run).** design/22af-findings.md, Finding 4, Blood Bowl: "good players make every safe off-ball move first and save the gamble for last... Structure the turn so off-ball positioning is free/safe before the shot, and the shot is the possession-ending gamble taken last." Idea bank row 24 in BUILD.md carries it. Finding 5 adds the two rules it needs to work: off-ball pieces get a small visible job each turn, and all-passive coverage must lose (Aaron 08-02: "I LOVE THIS", every incentive visible on screen).

**What the finding does not say, stated on file:** V0.md, "FULL SETUP IS RULED" block: "22af F4 names the SHAPE (safe setup before the gamble) and never picks a NUMBER." Blood Bowl moves every player every turn; the shipped July rule moved one; both were "legitimate readings". The number went to Aaron, who said "all pieces move free" (08-11), then "full range" (08-18), and on 09-05 walked it back to one free move a turn.

### (d) One matchup per possession

**Verdict on file: ADAPT, moderate confidence.** design/22af-findings.md, Finding 6, Strat-O-Matic Basketball: "never gives all ten players a job. A card flip picks the featured player and the possession resolves through his duel with his defender. Everyone else is context." Caveat carried in the file: source is the publisher's product page, one of three checkers dissented, and the same game's court-spacing claims all failed checking, "take the spotlight idea and nothing else." Idea bank row 26. design/d37-defense-findings.md, Q3, adds that every basketball design found lands in one of three buckets: a declared call resolved abstractly, assignments plus ratings, or auto-positioned bodies under a standing setting. None moves pieces one at a time.

Then B5: "third strike, CLOSED FOREVER per the brief, alternating stays, playtesting owns it now, and no future session gets to reopen it as a research question." **What the archive does say about a turn's grammar, all of it design reasoning or shipped law rather than research:**

Their turn: lights down on the sky and chrome, floor stays lit ("the player has to watch what moves"). A question card owns the whole screen.
- DESIGN.md section 12b: the turn tray ("love the tray"), the player menu over a radial wheel (his pick, because the menu is priced from the engine's own functions), and the standing admission, unsolved since 08-17: "it's still unclear when it's your turn, maybe that's really all that the screen needs to show 'your turn!' and a player menu showing your options."

- design/d37-defense-findings.md, Q5: Football, Tactics and Glory's turn is "blocks of exactly 3 actions" per side, defense automatic during the attacker's block. That is a per-turn grammar fact, found in passing, never evaluated.

## 4. Recommendation: is a new comparative run worth his time?

**Yes, one narrow run, and it should not block the option round.** Three reasons.

First, the closure in design/22af-findings.md, B5 ("no future session gets to reopen it as a research question") was written about a different question: alternating possessions versus whole-team turns versus simultaneous resolution, at the level of which SIDE acts. The 09-05 direction keeps alternating possessions. Its question is inside the possession: one piece at a time, offense then defense, and what a single turn may contain. That question has never been put to the research, and the one time the shape came up (Guild Ball and Kill Team's alternating activations) the brief had forbidden it. Reopening the closed question would be wrong; asking the new one is not reopening it.

Second, the evidence the archive already has for and against the 09-05 shape is thin on both sides. For it: the classic one-for-one exchange measured fair on 08-10 (V0.md D33), and two shipped skirmish games that alternate one unit at a time. Against it: Hero Academy and Magic Arena on snippet-level sources, and the "taps per beat" argument, which has never been counted for a one-move turn. A panel can draw concepts from this, but numbers for the brief's Numbers section (taps, seconds, longest wait) would be guesses.

Third, the skill's own decision rule says a comparative run works in groups of three or four questions, one lane each (.claude/skills/design-research-brief/SKILL.md, Part 1b). Four questions is the right size. The 09-05 panel has already run (TODO.md row 239 note), so the run should feed the option round's second draft, not gate the first.

**The four questions, each naming the decision it changes and the games to study:**

1. **What does one turn contain in shipped turn-based sports games, and what does the other side get after a move-then-act?** Study Blood Bowl 2 and 3 (move or act, the rationed move-then-hit, the turnover), Football, Tactics and Glory (three actions a block, what a single action is), Hoop League Tactics (its offense side, which the archive never read), Frozen Cortex, Basketball Classics, Draft Day Sports. Demand a recommendation for "move then shoot: yes, no, or rationed", with the reason and what the defense gets. Decision changed: row 239's open half (move-then-shoot), and the 08-02 open item on the pass-then-catch-and-shoot beat.

2. **How do alternating-turn phone games make the hand-off and the clock impossible to miss?** Study chess apps (the side to move, the last move lit, the clock of the side to move, the board that does not flip for the passive side), Fire Emblem and Advance Wars (the phase banner, spent units greyed), Into the Breach (reveal, then your move), Hearthstone (the turn banner and the rope), Pokemon (the four-choice row and the message box that narrates). Demand verbatim player complaints about "did not know it was my turn". Decision changed: rows 234, 236 and 242 (the possession callout, the clock, the announcer line), and DESIGN.md section 3's "on a possession flip (and ONLY a flip) the slam calls it".

3. **What does a possession cost on a phone in the shipped games nearest ours: taps, seconds, decisions each side, longest wait, and what reviews call tedious?** Study Hoop League Tactics, Football, Tactics and Glory mobile, Retro Bowl, Tecmo Bowl (the whole play, not only the pick), Blood Bowl 3 on console, chess apps for their per-move tap count. Numbers over adjectives; App Store and Steam reviews linked, not paraphrased. Decision changed: the Numbers targets every concept in the panel has to hit, and row 227's setup-clock question.

4. **How do 3D board games at phone size show the piece in hand, the legal squares, and whose move, without a zoom that hides the rest of the board?** Study 3D chess apps (Chess Ultra, Pure Chess, chess.com's 3D board), Into the Breach on phone, Fire Emblem on phone, and Hoop League Tactics' court camera. Decision changed: row 241 (the 3D-chess presentation) and row 237 (the zoom that hides pass targets), which touch the playing camera in DESIGN.md section 9.

An optional fifth, only if a lane is free: **how do sports games phrase play-by-play in one line** (NBA Street, NBA Jam, Blood Bowl's commentary, Madden's play-by-play), for row 242's announcer readout. It is copy, not structure, so it can wait.

What the run must NOT ask: whether to keep alternating possessions (closed, and he is keeping them); the defense's formation-pick answer (on file, section 1a and 1b); the play lists (chosen).

## 5. Where the 09-05 direction contradicts a locked decision

Listed plainly, with the section, so he can rule. "Contradicts" means the built or written rule says one thing and his 09-05 words say another; where they agree or merely fill a gap, it says so.

1. **The formation pick at every dead-ball inbound.** DESIGN.md section 3 (the Method B block): "the defense picks and reveals its setup FIRST and the offense picks seeing it" at every inbound; DESIGN.md section 12b: the turn tray opens with SETUPS; V0.md B16: "the offense's setup list is CONTEXTUAL BY DEAD-BALL SPOT" (baseline offers Box and 4-Low, sideline offers Zipper, made basket offers the backcourt inbound). His 09-05 words (row 238): picked once "at the beginning of each quarter or half or game", changed only by a timeout. Two consequences to rule on: the every-inbound ritual goes, and the spot-contextual menus cannot exist if the play is picked before any spot is known. The Up the Floor proposals in V0.md (two-part advance, set-back-or-press, always-three-universal) were all built on the every-inbound pick.

2. **No pick after the jump ball.** V0.md B16, 08-16, his own confirmation: "shouldn't there be a pick after the tip off too? ...ohh no that's wrong, there isn't, right?" CONFIRMED: none, "the ritual belongs to dead balls and the tip is a live ball". His 09-05 words: "in the beginning of the game after the jump ball... there should be a choose your offensive strategy." The 08-16 confirmation is reversed. The pregame huddle variant filed in that row is the nearest thing on the shelf.

3. **Every off-ball player moves free, at full range.** V0.md D37, his 08-11 ruling: "It should be all pieces move free"; DESIGN.md section 3, his 08-18 ruling: "lets give everyone full range and that's it". His 09-05 words (row 239): "we get rid of the whole everybody moves every time", keeping "movement of the person with the ball or somebody off the ball" free, which reads as one free move a turn. The rule count goes from up to four free moves to one.

4. **The free move draws no defensive answer.** V0.md D33, shipped 08-11 and locked into DESIGN.md section 3: "the free step draws NO defensive slide; the defense answers the main action only." His 09-05 words: "you can move, you can pass. Then the defense gets their turn to move." Under his direction the defense answers every offensive move, which is the rule D33 was written to prevent (the fear then: "either the defence answers each shuffle and no possession ever ends, or it answers none"). Note that DESIGN.md section 4's classic rule 1 ("after each offensive action, slide one defender one square") is the shape he is now describing, so this is a return to the classic one-for-one, not a new idea; the file still prints both rules.

5. **The order: free moves, then the slide, then the action.** DESIGN.md section 3: "the slide moves to after-setup-before-action"; section 12b: the tray reads SETUPS, BALL IN, FREE MOVES, SLIDE, ACTION; V0.md D37, his 08-12 theory: "offense has full team free movement and the defense has 1 person slide (full motion) and then offense has an action." His 09-05 words: one offensive thing, one defensive thing, back and forth, and his own doubt about where the shoot or pass falls. Row 239 says outright it "replaces the phase ladder". Section 12b's "love the tray" ruling is what it replaces.

6. **Defense first and visible, at the pick.** DESIGN.md section 3 and design/d37-defense-findings.md Q4 and design/22ai-findings.md all lock: defense picks first, the pick is shown. His 09-05 words: "I don't know if defense should see what offense is doing first or if both teams should pick blindly in the beginning." Not a contradiction yet, a reopening; the research's answer is on file (section 1a), and the hidden-simultaneous version was rejected by name (Tecmo Bowl).

7. **The hand-off is called only on a possession flip.** DESIGN.md section 3: "On a possession flip (and ONLY a flip) the slam calls it: YOUR TURN / THEY'RE UP." His 09-05 words (row 236): "Sometimes defense moves, you don't even realize it's your turn again. That should never happen." Under one-move turns the hand-off happens many times a possession, so the "ONLY a flip" clause and the 08-18 ruling behind it (a big callout on flips, a quiet strip otherwise) need re-ruling.

8. **The 24 second clock is per turn.** DESIGN.md section 3: "Turn-based with a :24 shot clock per turn"; BUILD.md changelog 07-23: "Defense clock is now :24 too". The panel brief describes it as a clock for the possession. Under one-move turns the two readings diverge sharply (a 24 per side per move would make a possession minutes long). His words on 09-05 ask for one clock, big, top right, with a buzzer. Which clock it is (possession or turn) is his to rule; the file currently says per turn.

9. **Substitutions at quarter breaks.** DESIGN.md section 7: "Substitutions at quarter breaks (bench = M-later)". His 09-05 words (row 238): "Timeouts will eventually be used for substitutions as well." A gap plus a soft contradiction; timeouts have no rule anywhere in DESIGN.md today.

10. **The research's standing advice against rebuilding the turn.** Not a DESIGN.md lock, but filed twice as guidance: design/22af-findings.md A7 and BUILD.md idea bank row 32: "if playtests show the waiting player disengages... give the DEFENDER something to do inside the question beat... not to rebuild the turn system." And design/d37-defense-findings.md's rejection of "several small reaction windows". His 09-05 direction rebuilds the turn inside the possession. Since both pieces of advice are labelled reasoning or snippet-level, he can overrule them without contradicting evidence; he should do it out loud.

**Where 09-05 agrees with the locks, so nothing needs ruling:** alternating possessions (locked, kept); live-ball continuation with no reset on steals and rebounds (V0.md D37, his own rule, and his 09-05 "unless it's a fast break"); the player menu over a wheel (DESIGN.md section 12b, and his Pokemon choice row is a menu); the lights-down that leaves the floor lit (DESIGN.md section 3, and his "that movement should be zoomed in on, highlighted"); the eight-tile guarding and closed lanes (DESIGN.md section 4, untouched by his words); the screen carrying only the beat (DESIGN.md section 8a, extended 09-05 by his words); the rulings in DESIGN.md section 8a from the same playthrough (jump-ball limit, clock only during play, timed-out answer, inbound clock).
