The court

Fifteen squares long, eight wide. The shaded rectangles are the keys. Squares are called by letter and number, like a spreadsheet: the ball handler "at G4". Cut the ten chips out along the dotted lines, or use coins. The rim is the small circle at each end.

The rules

Your turn (offense)

  1. One free move of a teammate without the ball, up to his number of squares. You can skip it.
  2. Then one thing with the ball: dribble (up to his number of squares), pass, or shoot. That ends your turn. Saying "end turn" also ends it and still spends a ball.
  3. Crossover: dribbling into a square next to a defender who is between you and the rim brings up a question. Right, and the defender gets a question to stay in front. If he misses, you are past him: ONE MORE, a shot or a pass right away, no defensive turn. The man you beat cannot contest that shot.
  4. Passing: free if 3 squares or less, or up to 6 with no defender on the line. Otherwise a question for the passer (7 or more is hard).
  5. Shooting: a question. Within about 2 squares of the rim is a layup (easy). Outside the arc is a three (hard). Everything else is mid-range (medium). A defender next to you and between you and the rim makes it one step harder.

Their turn (defense)

  1. Move one defender up to his number of squares. Or, if a defender is already next to the ball handler, go for the steal instead. Never both. "End turn" is allowed.
  2. Steal: the defender's question. Wrong, he is moved one square to the side and the handler has an open lane. Right, the handler's question to hold on. Both right, nothing happens. Defender right and handler wrong, stolen: live ball.
  3. Three seconds: a defender may stand in the key for two of his team's defensive turns. On the third, he must step out, or the game moves him to the nearest square outside the key and that was the step. Use the KEY chip as the counter.

The balls (the shot clock)

  1. On a live ball: two turns to get over half court, then three to shoot. The moment the ball crosses, any unused crossing turn is gone. After a made basket you are already over: three turns.
  2. Miss the crossing: HALF-COURT VIOLATION. Miss the shot: SHOT CLOCK VIOLATION. Other team's ball. An offensive rebound gives two more turns. A timeout resets nothing.
  3. After a made basket: both teams place their five in their plays at the other end (no walk-up); the point guard has the ball. The offense may choose which square past half court he starts on. After a steal or a rebound, play on from where everyone stands.

Clocks (use a phone stopwatch)

  • Offense: 24 seconds to take a turn. Defense: 10 seconds. Run out, and that turn is gone.
  • A question: 15 seconds to answer.

Questions on paper

Recommended for the first afternoon: a die and a ten count

Roll one die. Easy passes on 1 to 5, medium on 1 to 4, hard on 1 to 3. Before every roll, count to ten out loud. The count stands in for the fifteen seconds a real question takes, so the seconds you write down mean something.

If you want real trivia

One person reads a question from any basketball trivia source, the other answers, fifteen seconds on the stopwatch. Slower to set up, closer to the game.

Before you start

  • Jump ball: flip a coin. The winner picks an offense (Horns, Five-Out, Box), the loser picks a defense first (Man to man, 2-3 zone) and shows it.
  • Play ten possessions. Time every one. Fill the tally sheet as you go, not after.

The three things only this afternoon can answer

1. Did the back and forth feel like chess or like ping-pong? Write the possession number where it first felt like either.

2. Two then three: too tight, right, or loose? Did anyone ever get a violation?

3. Did the defense ever want a second thing on its turn, or was one step enough?
