# The possession flow · the memo, 2026-09-05

Written for Aaron after his second 09-05 message, the one that pushed back:
*"Walk me through what a simple clean process would look like... do not stay
stuck on current structure or patterns, think inside and outside the box...
make the best not easiest decision."* Rows 238 and 239 carry his words.

How it was made: an archive scout read every research file for what is
already answered (scout.md); eight designers each drew the possession from a
different angle (concept-*.md); three critics judged every concept through a
first-time phone player, the tempo arithmetic, and the locked rules
(critique-*.md); this memo is the synthesis. The board with the frames:
<https://claude.ai/code/artifact/27ef4220-ee68-4d38-8779-d3abedade3c8>. Nothing is built.

## 1. His doubt, answered

> "The offense picks a player and moves them, then the defense picks a player
> and moves them and THEN the offense chooses to shoot or pass? And then the
> offense goes again to start it over? That feels confusing."

It feels confusing because that sentence has two kinds of offensive turn in
it, a moving turn and a shooting turn, with the defense wedged between them.
Every clean version the panel found does one thing: it makes every offensive
turn the same turn.

**The rule a player can repeat: one thing with the ball, then they get one
step, again, until you shoot.** Move, pass or shoot are the three things, and
shoot is on the row every time, so there is no "then shoot" stage and nothing
ever starts over. The defense's step is not a turn wedged in; it is the
answer to what you just did, made after seeing it, which is the one shape the
August defense research found reads as fair (Kill Team's answer move, Into
the Breach's informed small reaction).

Four things on the screen and nothing else: the announcer line at the top
saying what just happened; one clock, top right, in the colour of the side
on the clock; the court with the ball handler lit and the last move still
tinted on the floor (what chess apps do); the row of buttons at the bottom,
which exists only when it is your turn and whose header is the one place the
game says whose turn it is.

## 2. The candidates

**A · One thing, then their step (recommended).** Every offensive turn is
one of MOVE (any of your five, ball or not), PASS, SHOOT. Every defensive
turn is one step by one defender (HOLD, STEP, STEAL when adjacent). Move then
shoot: never in one turn; move, they step, shoot. Beating your man still
matters because a beaten defender recovers one tile only, so help must come
and someone comes open. Numbers: offense 4 to 6 decisions, defense 3 to 5,
about 14 taps, 35 to 50 seconds with one card, longest wait the 15 second
card. The strongest objection that survived: six to ten hand-offs a
possession, fine online and against the computer, unproven on one shared
phone. Merged from the chess, matchup and Pokemon concepts.

**B · Move one, then pass. Or shoot.** His own words read the other way
("they can still move and then pass"): one turn is a free move by any one
player and then a pass, or a shot instead; the defense steps once after the
whole turn. A shot is a turn by itself, from where the ball already is, so
the defense always answers it. Half the hand-offs of A; the row changes shape
mid-turn; the best fit for a shared phone.

**C · Three moves, then shoot.** A with a visible cap: three balls in the
strip, one spent per move, one defensive step after each, then SHOOT alone.
The most learnable shape; the same skeleton every possession; three moves
cannot cross the floor on a steal, so the fast break needs its own rule.

**D · Build, answer, finish.** No loop: one offensive move, one defensive
move seeing the price of every shot on the floor, then the shot or one pass
to the man who shoots. Seven to eleven taps, two hand-offs. It removes the
back and forth he asked for and breaks on live balls (all ten at the wrong
end). Pick it on purpose or not at all.

**E · Lock and play.** Both sides pick at once, then the beat plays; a guess
moves a price one step, the card decides. Killed for this question: he asked
for a defense that gets a turn to respond, and the research rejected hidden
simultaneous calls by name. Its price tags on every piece (OPEN, TRAFFIC,
CONTESTED) go into A.

**Killed outright:** the play running itself as a film with read points
(deletes the free move he kept; needs every play authored; kept as A's
upgrade path, one scripted cut per exchange drawn at the pick), and real
time with the 24 running (not turn-based; its home is the streetball mode
the design file reserves as Blacktop).

## 2b. Madden, Rabbids and the role-playing games (his pushback, same day)

His third message: *"why not games like Madden and Rabbids as well? Or even
other turn based RPGs?"* The eight angles were shapes, not games. Madden fed
the play-runs concept and the play-pick screen (and the August play-call
research); Mario + Rabbids appears in the archive only as the team-turns
idea he offered himself on 08-01 and called "wack" (BUILD 22ad, the FL-2.6
toggle never built); the role-playing games appear nowhere. Three passes
went back through twenty-two games (lens-sports.md, lens-tactics.md,
lens-rpg.md). All three confirm A and none produces a new flow.

- Sports games: Madden's one controlled defender while the rest run the
  call is A's defensive turn; Blood Bowl rations move-then-act to once a
  turn and prices it, which A already has as the crossover; Football
  Tactics and Glory's reviews praise the three-pip counter and complain
  about the wait behind it, so C is retired on evidence; Retro Bowl cut the
  defense entirely.
- Rabbids and the tactics family: every one allows move-then-shoot and pays
  for it with a whole enemy turn or a pre-committed trap, the two answers
  already ruled out; the phone survivors (Into the Breach, Midnight Suns)
  are shapes inside A. Their gift is screen craft: preview on every dot,
  drag-to-move with the path drawn, light shading of guarded tiles.
- Role-playing games: one thing from a fixed menu, narrate, then the other
  side's menu rises, which is A; narration lands before the row rises
  (Pokemon), a NOW and NEXT chip pair beside the clock (Final Fantasy X),
  the price drawn on the path (Baldur's Gate 3); Paper Mario's guard press
  is the block card, add no more reflexes.

Four cuts to A that keep its grammar: the receiver ring is the pass button
(one tap); a wrong steal answer costs a blow-by; the picked play stays
faintly painted on the floor; the shot call runs across the card.

One rule for him, from Persona 5's One More: a right crossover answer earns
one more action at once with no defensive step, capped at one per turn,
never off a pass. It is the one place move-then-shoot is allowed, and it is
earned out loud.

## 3. Side by side

The table is on the board. In one line: A and C give the back and forth he
described with the fewest chores; B halves the hand-offs at the cost of a
two-part turn; D is fastest and loses the rally; E answers a different
question. Today's possession is about 18 taps and 45 to 75 seconds, most of
the taps before the defense sees anything.

## 4. The recommendation, and what he rules

A with the four cuts, One More on his desk, B held as the shared-phone fallback. C retired. Paper walkthrough first (a
printed court, two people, one afternoon), then a clickable mock on the real
renderer, then the game.

1. The grammar: A (one thing a turn) or B (move one, then pass).
2. The defense's step clock: the panel says 8 seconds. Today the defense has
   its own 24 (ruled 07-23, "both sides get to think"); this changes that.
3. The shared phone: lies between two people who tap the same screen, or
   each end gets its own row like the buzz race. The hand-off count matters
   only under the first.
4. The play pick: blind or sighted, and who picks first.
5. The steal: what a wrong answer costs the defender; the sports games say
   a blow-by.
6. One More: yes or no to the earned second action off a right crossover
   answer.

## 5. Research: answered, empty, never asked

**Answered in August:** the defense's fair answer (one informed step after
the offense acts, no prompts mid-beat); several small reaction windows read
as unbearable (thin sources: Magic Arena, Hero Academy); safe moves first and
the gamble last (Blood Bowl); one matchup per possession (Strat-O-Matic,
moderate); the play menu visible, one tap, defense first and shown. Turn
order at the level of which side acts came back empty three times and was
closed; the 09-05 question is inside the possession and was never asked.

**Never asked:** what a single turn may contain and what the other side gets
after move-then-act; how phone games make the hand-off and the clock
impossible to miss; what a possession costs in taps and seconds in the
shipped games nearest ours; how 3D board games at phone size show the piece
in hand without a zoom. His move-then-shoot doubt has been filed open since
08-02.

**Is a run worth his time?** Yes, one narrow run of four questions, and it
feeds the second draft rather than gating the paper walkthrough:

1. What does one turn contain in shipped turn-based sports games, and what
   does the other side get after move-then-act? Blood Bowl 2 and 3, Football
   Tactics and Glory, Hoop League Tactics (its offense side), Frozen Cortex,
   Basketball Classics, Draft Day Sports. Decision: row 239's move-then-shoot
   half and the 08-02 catch-and-shoot item.
2. How do alternating-turn phone games make the hand-off and the clock
   impossible to miss? Chess apps, Fire Emblem, Advance Wars, Into the
   Breach, Hearthstone, Pokemon. Decision: rows 234, 236, 242 and the "slam
   only on a flip" rule in DESIGN § 3.
3. What does a possession cost on a phone in the nearest shipped games:
   taps, seconds, decisions, longest wait, what reviews call tedious? Hoop
   League Tactics, Football Tactics and Glory, Retro Bowl, Tecmo Bowl, Blood
   Bowl 3, chess apps. Decision: the number targets every flow must hit, and
   row 227's setup clock.
4. How do 3D board games at phone size show the piece in hand, the legal
   squares and whose move, without a zoom that hides the board? Chess Ultra,
   Pure Chess, chess.com 3D, Into the Breach on phone, Hoop League Tactics'
   camera. Decision: rows 237 and 241, the playing camera in DESIGN § 9.

Not to be asked again: alternating possessions (closed, kept), the defense's
formation-pick answer (on file), the play lists (chosen).

## 6. Where 09-05 crosses a locked decision (his to rule)

1. The formation pick at every dead-ball inbound and its spot-by-spot menus
   (DESIGN § 3, § 12b): replaced by pick-once.
2. "No pick after the jump ball" (his 08-16 confirmation): reversed.
3. Every off-ball player moves free at full range (08-11, 08-18): now one
   free move.
4. The free move draws no defensive answer (08-11): now the defense answers
   every move.
5. Free moves, then the slide, then the action, and the step ladder he loved
   (§ 12b): replaced whole.
6. The slam calls the turn only on a possession flip (§ 3): under one-move
   turns the hand-off is many times a possession.
7. The 24 is per turn and the defense has its own 24 (§ 3, 07-23): the
   panel's clock is per possession, offense decision time only.
8. Substitutions at quarter breaks (§ 7): timeouts, later.
9. The research's filed advice not to rebuild the turn (reasoning, not
   evidence): he can overrule it out loud.
