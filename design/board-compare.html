Grounding the court

"sometimes it feels low budget, airy, weird, even the backboards the stantions, the numbers and stuff everywhere just floating and not anchored to anything with real feel and design"Aaron, 08-19

Your word for it was airy, and airiness turned out to be four specific things, each fixable in the renderer. Look at the close-up pair first: it is where the change lives.

What was actually wrong, named

  • The court ended at the sideline. A real court has floor out there, a darker band of the same wood, and that apron is what the coordinate letters are painted on. Without it the letters hung in the black and the whole floor read as a sticker on a background. The apron now carries them, with a lit lip where the deck meets the room and a soft shadow underneath.
  • The shadows were stickers. Every piece sat on a hard 35 percent grey ellipse of fixed size, no matter how high it was. A real shadow is darkest and tightest where the object touches the floor and spreads as it rises. It is now a gradient with an occlusion core, and it reads the piece's height, so a jumping player's shadow lets go of him.
  • The floor was paint swatches. Flat colour per tile is why it looked cheap. Planks now run the length of the room at a third of a tile wide, with staggered butt joints and one soft overhead sheen. Narrow enough that they never fight the grid the game is played on.
  • The numbers floated. White text on a curved body has nothing holding it. Each number now sits on a darkened jersey panel, which anchors it and buys contrast on every colourway.

Honest limits of pass one

  • The backboard and stanchion were already well built (padded base, A-frame boom, hanger arms) and I did not touch them this pass. If they still read thin to you, the likely culprit is the near-goal ghosting to 45 percent so it never blocks play, not the geometry. Say the word and it gets its own pass.
  • The pieces themselves are unchanged. They are lathe-turned figurines with flat shading; making them read as sculpted objects is a bigger job than grounding them, and it deserves its own comparison.
  • Frame rate held at 61fps in the headless phone viewport with the extra work, but that is a desktop GPU pretending to be a phone. Your iPhone is the real gate.
