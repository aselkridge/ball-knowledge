The Daily Five

Everything it does right now, in plain English, with real screenshots of the real thing. Read it, ask what you need to ask, and either send it live or tell me what to change.

One rack. Ten questions. Same ten for everybody, every day. Five shots you have to make, five you have to stop, and if you go a perfect ten you get one shot at naming a mystery player. It takes about two minutes.

It is finished and tested but not live — you said to hold it while you thought about changes, so it is sitting on a branch. Nothing below is a plan or a mock-up; every picture is the actual game, photographed at a desktop size and at phone size.

01The calendar on the menu

This is the thing you asked for: not another button in the row, but a calendar page pinned next to the logo that you tear off once a day.

{{PAIR_MENU}}

It shows today's real date. It leans about 6° off straight so it never reads as part of the tidy button stack, and it breathes gently until you touch it. Tapping it slams the words CLOCK IN! across the screen and shakes the title — the same trick the menu buttons use, so it feels like it belongs.

Once you have played

{{PAIR_DONE}}

It greys out, takes a green tick, and says BACK TOMORROW. It stops breathing and it will not slam a second time — a day you have finished is finished. It stays that way if you close the game and come back, and it re-arms on its own at midnight.

One difference between the two sizes, and it is your call

On a desktop the calendar sits beside the wordmark, sharing 119 pixels of height with it — which is what you asked for. On a phone there is not room, so it stacks above the logo instead, sharing nothing. Everything still fits on one screen without scrolling and it is comfortably big enough to tap.

I have not changed it, because a 198-pixel calendar will not fit next to a wordmark on a 390-pixel screen without one of them getting small. But most people will play this on a phone, so you should see it and decide rather than find out later.

02How a round actually goes

First five — make the shot

A half-court with five spots on it, working outward from under the basket. Answer the question, make the shot. Closer shots are easier questions and worth less; the logo shot is the hardest and pays most.

{{PAIR_R1}}

The colours are the game's own difficulty language, the same one the cards use everywhere else — green easy amber medium red hard and gold for legendary. Because gold and amber are genuinely close to each other on screen, the legendary spot also carries a ★, so it is never colour alone doing the work.

Miss, and you are told nothing

{{PAIR_MISS}}

One tap per card, no second go. Your wrong answer turns red and the other three say nothing at all. That was your ruling and I have kept it exactly.

But the reason behind that ruling is not built, and you need to know

The thinking was: don't show the answer, because the card comes back another day until you beat it. I found today that nothing actually remembers what you missed. The ten cards are picked purely from the date. The game stores two things — which day you played and your score — and nothing else.

So a card you got wrong comes back only by luck, exactly as often as it comes back for someone who got it right. As it stands a player can miss something and simply never find out. That may be fine — it does push people to go and look it up. But the justification for staying quiet was the card returning, and the card does not return. This is the biggest thing on this page and it is a decision, not a bug.

03The scorecard

{{PAIR_RECEIPT}}

Twenty-four points is the most anyone can score. The block in the middle is the bit that gets copied and pasted into a group chat — basketballs for shots you made, bricks for the ones you missed, shields for stops, alarms for the ones that got past you.

Something this screenshot caught today

The stops line was rendering as five hollow outlines instead of five shields — so a perfect ten looked, on the share, like you had stopped nobody. One of the four symbols is the only one Unicode treats as text rather than a picture unless you tell it otherwise. Fixed, and the screenshot above is the fixed version. It would have gone out with every bragging message reading as a zero.

Nine out of ten is not ten out of ten

{{PAIR_LOCKED}}

One miss and the bonus stays shut, and the scorecard says so plainly rather than just hiding the button.

04The Heat Check

{{PAIR_HEAT}}

Go a perfect ten and you get one mystery player. No multiple choice — you type the name. Four clues, and the longer you hold out the more it pays: 6 points on the first clue, then 4, then 3, then 2. The first clue always contains a real number so it is a puzzle rather than a guess, and no clue ever says the player's name.

The typing is forgiving on purpose. Micheal Jordn is accepted. So is just Jordan if only one player in the pool has that surname — and if two do, it asks you to be more specific and does not charge you for it. Nicknamed players answer to their ordinary name.

The mystery player is fixed for the day and rolls over at midnight. There are 65 of them (51 NBA, 14 WNBA), so it is about two months before anyone sees a repeat.

06What has actually been proven

There is an automatic test that drives the real game in a real browser and checks 53 things, every one of them passing. That includes playing all ten cards, missing on purpose, sweeping, and unlocking the bonus.

A test nobody has attacked is not worth much, so I broke the game five different ways on purpose to confirm the tests actually notice:

What I sabotagedDid the tests catch it?
Made the daily set random instead of fixed to the date caught — 3 failures
Let every league back into the questions caught — 201 out-of-scope cards found
Made a miss reveal the right answercaught
Removed the ★ from the legendary spotcaught
Painted the legendary spot the wrong colourcaught

Two things worth admitting about that. The first time I tried removing the ★ the tests passed — I had edited the wrong file, so nothing was actually broken. And until today the tests only ever looked at a desktop-sized screen, which is how the phone layout difference in section 01 went unnoticed. The phone is now checked too.

07What I need from you

Q1The missed card that never comes back blocks shipping, in my view

Section 02. Either we build the memory so a missed card really does return until you beat it, or we accept that some answers are never revealed and the player has to go looking. My recommendation: ship as-is and add the memory later. Never revealing anything is a defensible, distinctive choice, and "go and find out" is the whole spirit of the game — but you should be choosing it knowingly rather than inheriting it from a comment that was wrong.

Q2The stamp stacks above the title on a phone your call

Section 01. My recommendation: leave it. It is big, it is the first thing you see, and it does not push anything off the screen. The alternative is shrinking it to sit beside the wordmark, which loses exactly the presence you asked for.

Q3Does the share text need a link? your call

Right now the copied block is the title, the date, your score and the symbols. It does not include a link to the game. For twenty friends in a group chat that is probably fine; for anything beyond them, a link is how it spreads.

Q4Two minutes a day — is that enough? worth thinking about

Ten cards is a short visit. That is deliberate for a daily habit, but it is the one thing I cannot test for you. It is the sort of thing the twenty will tell you in a week.

08What is not in this

  • No sound. The Daily Five is silent. You called the heat sound "cool, not essential" and the same goes here.
  • No streaks. Nothing counts consecutive days or shows a best score. It remembers today and nothing before it.
  • No leaderboard. Comparing scores happens in the group chat, not in the game.
  • Cards are not verified yet. This is the honest one. No answer in the whole game has ever been checked against its source — that work is queued and separate, and it applies to every mode, not just this one.
