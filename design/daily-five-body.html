The Daily Five

Everything it does right now, in plain English, with real screenshots of the real thing. Read it, ask what you need to ask, and either send it live or tell me what to change.

One rack. Ten questions. Same ten for everybody, every day. Five shots you have to make, five you have to stop, and if you go a perfect ten you get one shot at naming a mystery player. It takes about two minutes.

It is finished, tested and waiting on your merge — it sits on a branch, and main is the live site. Nothing below is a plan or a mock-up; every picture is the actual game, photographed at a desktop size and at phone size.

What was wrong with it, because it is worth knowing

The tick was green. On the calendar, green means "caught up late". So the menu was showing someone who finished today the mark for having missed it — two screens one tap apart using the same colour for opposite things. That is the same mistake as red meaning both "worth 3" and "hard", and I made this one myself a few hours earlier in the same session.

Both screens now ask the same function for the mark and for the shape, so they cannot drift apart again.

One difference between the two sizes — you have ruled on this

On a desktop the calendar sits beside the wordmark, sharing 119 pixels of height with it — which is what you asked for. On a phone there is not room, so it stacks above the logo instead, sharing nothing. Everything still fits on one screen without scrolling and it is comfortably big enough to tap.

A 198-pixel calendar will not fit next to a wordmark on a 390-pixel screen without one of them getting small. You said you were fine with it, so it stays — and the automatic checks now assert the stacked layout, so changing it has to be deliberate rather than accidental.

02How a round actually goes

First five — make the shot

A half-court with five spots on it, working outward from under the basket. Answer the question, make the shot. Closer shots are easier questions and worth less; the logo shot is the hardest and pays most.

{{PAIR_R1}}

The colours are the game's own difficulty language, the same one the cards use everywhere else — green easy amber medium red hard and gold for legendary. Because gold and amber are genuinely close to each other on screen, the legendary spot also carries a ★, so it is never colour alone doing the work.

Miss, and you are told nothing

{{PAIR_MISS}}

One tap per card, no second go. Your wrong answer turns red and the other three say nothing at all. That was your ruling and I have kept it exactly.

The reason behind that ruling is not built — shipping anyway, on your call

The thinking was: don't show the answer, because the card comes back another day until you beat it. I found today that nothing actually remembers what you missed. The ten cards are picked purely from the date. The game stores two things — which day you played and your score — and nothing else.

So a card you got wrong comes back only by luck, exactly as often as it comes back for someone who got it right. As it stands a player can miss something and simply never find out. That may be fine — it does push people to go and look it up. But the justification for staying quiet was the card returning, and the card does not return. You chose to ship as-is and log it, so it is a feature to build, not a defect. Written up in V0 with the catch noted: building it properly breaks "everyone gets the same ten", which is the mode's whole social point, so it likely wants to be a separate make-up rack rather than seeded into the shared daily.

03The scorecard

{{PAIR_RECEIPT}}

Twenty-four points is the most anyone can score. The block in the middle is the bit that gets copied and pasted into a group chat — basketballs for shots you made, bricks for the ones you missed, shields for stops, alarms for the ones that got past you.

Something this screenshot caught today

The stops line was rendering as five hollow outlines instead of five shields — so a perfect ten looked, on the share, like you had stopped nobody. One of the four symbols is the only one Unicode treats as text rather than a picture unless you tell it otherwise. Fixed, and the screenshot above is the fixed version. It would have gone out with every bragging message reading as a zero.

04Your streak

The streak button — and why you had to ask

It is the small pill at the top of the Daily Five screen, next to the date. It shows how many days you are on and it opens the calendar. It sits there before you play, not just on the scorecard, because the streak is the reason to come back.

{{STREAK_BTN}}

You asking "what is the streak button?" WAS the usability test

The first version was a hairline pill in muted grey sitting right next to a line of muted grey caption text. It read as a label, not a control — which is exactly why you had to ask what it was. That is better evidence than any opinion I could have offered.

It now has a filled surface, a calendar icon, the number in the game's orange, and it lifts on hover. The screenshot above is the fixed version.

{{MARKS_SIX}}

All six, at the size they actually appear. Nothing here relies on you telling gold from green — each row and each column has a different shape too.

{{PAIR_CAL}}

Two separate questions — how far did you get, and did you do it on the day — so the shape and the colour each answer one. Neither has to carry the whole message.

PlayedSwept the tenAll eleven
On the day Gold tick Gold star, filled Gold crown, filled
Caught up later Green tick Green star, hollow Green crown, hollow

The caught-up marks are the same shapes, drawn hollow — not different awards. You did the same thing; you just got there a day late. The tick is the one exception to the hollow rule and can't help it: a tick is a line, there's nothing inside it to empty out, so it carries the colour alone.

A day you have not played shows as a dashed empty box, and you can tap it and play it. Catching up repairs your streak — that is the whole reason missed days are playable. Days in the future are not tappable.

One thing I decided, so you can overrule it

Your streak does not read as broken just because you have not played today yet. At nine in the morning it still shows yesterday's number. It only breaks when a day passes with nothing in it.

You asked: is art needed?

Not for these. A five-point star and a three-point crown are flat vector shapes with clean silhouettes — that is exactly the kind of thing code does well, the same category as the court, the shot spots and the HUD. They are drawn as geometry, and what you see above is what ships.

Where it would change: if you want a painted crown — texture, metal, depth, something with a hand in it — that is illustration, and hand-coding has a hard ceiling there. I would be building a lumpy approximation and calling it a crown. If that is the direction, tell me and I will spec exactly what to source (subject, style, framing, transparent background) rather than guess at it.

The one real constraint either way: the crown and the star are both gold. They have to be tellable apart by shape, not colour, because at 14 pixels gold is gold. That is why the crown also carries a ring around its day.

05The clock

You asked whether there was a timer. There wasn't — you could sit on a card and look the answer up. There is now, and your note about slow readers changed how it works.

The rule: 12 seconds to think, plus however long the card takes to read

My first version was a flat 25 seconds for every card. Then I checked what that actually meant for someone who reads slowly. Across all 711 cards in the daily pool, here is how much time a slow reader had left to think after finishing reading:

CardWordsThink-time left on a flat 25s
Typical2015.0s
Long3010.0s
The longest in the bank53 −1.5s — they can't finish reading it

A flat clock isn't one rule, it's a different rule per card, and the person it punishes hardest is exactly the one you flagged. So the clock is now 12 seconds of thinking, plus reading time priced at a slow reader's pace. Everybody gets the same 12 seconds to actually think, on every card, whatever their speed.

In practice that's 17 to 39 seconds depending on the card, median 22. Note that a short card now gets less than the flat 25 — generous where it has to be, tighter where it doesn't. And it's still nowhere near enough to switch apps, type a question and read a result.

Running out counts as a miss — and it still tells you nothing, same as a wrong tap. It doesn't mark a red answer either, because you never picked one.

The Heat Check gets one clock for the whole round, not one per clue — 25 seconds to think plus the reading time of the clues you actually open. Asking for another clue costs points and time, so it's a real decision; but it hands back the seconds needed to read that clue, so a slow reader isn't paying twice.

One loophole I had to close

The streak button sits at the top of the screen and was tappable mid-question — so opening your calendar over a live card would have been a free pause to go and look something up. The clock doesn't stop for anything, so the button is switched off while a card is live.

06The Heat Check

{{PAIR_HEAT}}

Go a perfect ten and you get one mystery player. No multiple choice — you type the name. Four clues, and the longer you hold out the more it pays: 6 points on the first clue, then 4, then 3, then 2. The first clue always contains a real number so it is a puzzle rather than a guess, and no clue ever says the player's name.

The typing is forgiving on purpose. Micheal Jordn is accepted. So is just Jordan if only one player in the pool has that surname — and if two do, it asks you to be more specific and does not charge you for it. Nicknamed players answer to their ordinary name.

The mystery player is fixed for the day and rolls over at midnight. There are 65 of them (51 NBA, 14 WNBA), so it is about two months before anyone sees a repeat.

08What has actually been proven

09What I need from you

Q1The 36 mis-tagged cards needs a ruling before it can be fixed properly

The daily is exact now, but it got there by dropping the whole "evergreen" bucket rather than by fixing the 36 cards. Re-tagging them needs you first: there is no ABA league in the game, and adding one puts ABA in the league picker players see. FIBA could sit under Flags or Overseas; the Globetrotters under Street or Early Black Basketball. My recommendation: add an ABA league. It is a real defunct pro league, twelve cards are about it, and every non-NBA/WNBA league is already waiting outside V0 — so it lands in the waiting room where it belongs. Once tagged, the evergreen cards come back to the daily in one line.

Q2The Daily Five's song swap it if you disagree

It was playing the menu track. It now has its own: Ketsa, "Follow My Soul" — one of the two you cast as boombox-only, so nothing new had to be sourced. It is the calmer of the pair and the daily is a two-minute ritual rather than a game. One line to swap for "Cursed Without". A genuinely new song is a sourcing job, not a coding one — Ketsa has more albums under the same licence and I can spec what to look for, but I cannot write one.

Q4Two minutes a day — is that enough? worth thinking about

Ten cards is a short visit. That is deliberate for a daily habit, but it is the one thing I cannot test for you. It is the sort of thing the twenty will tell you in a week.

10What is not in this

  • No sound effects. It has a song now, but no swish, clang or buzzer. You called the heat sound "cool, not essential" and the same goes here.
  • No best-score record. The calendar counts days and perfect days, but nothing tracks your highest score or an average.
  • No leaderboard. Comparing scores happens in the group chat, not in the game.
  • Cards are not verified yet. This is the honest one. No answer in the whole game has ever been checked against its source — that work is queued and separate, and it applies to every mode, not just this one.
