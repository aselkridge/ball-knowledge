FLOW

Where the matching happens

The same tags get compared at six different moments, and the rules aren’t identical at each one.

You pick a league

7 leagues are declared, only NBA and WNBA are unlocked

The other five say “in the lab” — they are gated on data, not code

You pick decades

your decades vs each player’s decades‑played

A player is dealable if he played in any decade you picked

You pick question packs

packs are extra question sources you opt into

Packs only ever add cards. They can never remove one

The game deals your five

league must match exactly · position fills the lineup · rarity sets the stars

You cannot get a college player in an NBA game

The game draws a question

difficulty and league and decade — all three must pass

Plus “haven’t asked it yet this game”

The game shows a player

the one career line on the record

Not decade-aware. Pick the 1990s, get Jordan’s whole-career average

GAPS

What’s broken

Everything here is measured, not suspected.

Not connected2,446 accolade claims on player cards, and not one carries a source.

Every player has them — “8x NBA champion”, “led the nation in scoring”. That is more individual factual claims than the entire question bank has cards, they print on the player card, and there is no evidence trail for any of them. All the sourcing work so far was scoped to questions; this whole category was never in it and was unmeasured until now.

Not connected883 cards know which players they’re about. The game never looks.

Those cards name 332 different players. The code that picks a question checks difficulty, league and decade, then stops. This is why questions about your own team don’t come up more often — the feature was written down but never wired in.

Not connected373 cards point at evidence that isn’t there.

Every one came from question run 3, whose merge wrote the run’s internal scratch ID into the card instead of the real fact ID. 37 can be relinked mechanically; the other 336 never had their facts saved to disk at all and need re-sourcing.

Not connectedNo player has decade-by-decade numbers. (0 of 744)

So picking a single decade still shows a player’s whole-career average. The fix is per-season packages storing totals, not averages — you cannot average averages.

Not connectedNeither players nor questions have a permanent name tag.

Players are filed under their name and nothing else (0 of 744 have an ID); no card has one (0 of 1,526). Two men are already in the database twice under different spellings, and online play identifies a question by counting positions in a list.

Partly thereTwo ways to cite a source: 1,326 cards use a fact ID, 200 use a bare web link.

Same job, two systems — which is how you end up with a fact and a URL that are the same evidence with nothing joining them. Every card should point at a fact ID; the URLs live on the fact.

Partly thereSource reliability is recorded on 243 of 1,128 facts and on no card at all.

Tier 1 is the actual record; tier 2 needs two independent sources agreeing; tier 3 is an index you search from, never something you cite. Nothing in the game can currently answer “how solid is this card?”

Partly there191 category labels for 1,526 cards — and they print on screen.

47 are used exactly once, and 15 pairs are the same thing spelled two ways. Needs a short approved list, mapped by review so no meaning is lost.

Partly there209 players have an average with no totals behind it.

608 players have a points-per-game figure but only 406 also have the total points and games it came from. Without those you cannot recombine a player’s numbers across eras — you cannot average averages. The totals have to be mined.

Partly thereFact IDs follow four different schemes at once (32 prefixes).

Leagues (nba-), competitions (oly-, wc-), topics (w-, 350 of them), individual people (petrovic-, sabonis-) and even a research run (v5-). Readable, but not a system — you cannot predict an ID or validate one.

Partly thereTeams are 337 free-text strings with no registry.

And franchises move and rename — the Seattle SuperSonics became the OKC Thunder, and “Charlotte Hornets” refers to two different franchises. A bare string will eventually lie about history.

Partly thereThe off-court flavour tag exists in the design and on 0 cards.

Specced as its own opt-in, never applied to anything.
