FL-2.5

CPU opponent

Shipped

Heuristic board AI layered over the existing rules engine on an input-layer contract — the CPU chooses among engine-computed options and never gets its own rules. Its "knowledge" is an accuracy dial per difficulty tier, driving tap-battles and timing meters alike, with human-paced think time so it never reads a card faster than a person could.

In plain termsYou can play alone. The computer isn't cheating — it picks from the same legal moves you get, and it's beatable because it gets questions wrong like anyone else.

Shipped today — gameplay fixes from your playtest

The answer beat

Live

Answer buttons are now marked correct at creation via a dataset flag instead of matched by text at resolution — duplicate or near-duplicate choice strings could previously light the wrong button. The correct answer pops bright green with a scale animation, a wrong pick shakes red, the verdict word slams in, and the hold extended 1400ms → 1800ms. That same 1800 is mirrored in the netcode's card handler so online stays in lockstep.

In plain termsYou said some questions didn't light up the right answer and that getting one right should feel like a moment. Both fixed — and the reason it was fragile is that the old code found the right button by comparing text, which breaks when two answers read similarly.

Coach timing — and no coach online

Live

The first coach tip now fires the moment the game screen mounts, before the jumbotron and jump ball, safe because the entire tip-off chain runs on pausable fTimeout deadlines that suspend and resume with remaining milliseconds. The 28 July pause fix had deferred it until the player's first offensive selection, which delayed the greeting by minutes whenever the CPU won the tip. Separately, the coach watcher now exits immediately in online games.

In plain termsThe coach says hello right away now. And your instinct about online was right — worse than you thought. Tips were appearing in online games without pausing anything, so they burned your live clock while you read. The coach is gone from online entirely; your first solo game still teaches you everything.

In plain termsSkills are instructions, and instructions can be drifted from. This is code that says no. It proved itself the same day it was built: while applying results I recreated the exact volatile-easy violation we'd just cleaned up, and the script failed my merge and made me do it properly.
