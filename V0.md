## GATE 1 HAS TWO HALVES NOW — RULED by Aaron, 2026-08-07

> *"is that 1000 wide enough or is it 1000 who was the mvp questions... maybe
> 1000 total questions isn't the request, maybe the request is build as many
> questions as possible, but make sure we have at least 1000 that can cover 20
> people playing 20 times each and getting varying questions."*

**He is right that a raw total is the wrong target, and the arithmetic backs
him.** 1,000 says nothing about what a player experiences. Two banks of 1,000
can feel completely different: one where every card is a Finals MVP question,
and one spread across leagues, eras, difficulties and question shapes.

**The number that matters is per-player exposure, not bank size.** Twenty people
playing twenty games each is not 400 cards, it is twenty separate experiences of
about twenty games. What each of them feels is how often something comes back.
That depends on three things the total does not capture:
- **how many cards a game actually deals** (needs measuring from the real game,
  not estimated),
- **how the pool is SLICED** — a player who picks NBA + 1990s + hard is not
  drawing from 1,000, they are drawing from whatever that intersection holds,
  which today is a small fraction,
- **rotation** — see BUILD.md § 6 · 22ag, filed the same day.

**The likely reframe, for Aaron to rule on:**
1. **Keep 1,000 as a floor, not a target.** It stays the launch gate.
2. **Add a SPREAD requirement**: a minimum per league x era x difficulty cell,
   so the thousand cannot all be one kind of question. The verified-pack gate
   already does exactly this shape at 25 per league x difficulty; this extends
   it to eras and question shape.
3. **Uncap the ceiling.** The real instruction is *build as many as possible*,
   and every question banked beyond the floor improves rotation forever.
4. **Measure the actual experience** before picking numbers: deal 20 simulated
   games per player profile through the real picker and count repeats.
   `gate-check.mjs` already drives the real picker across every league x decade
   x tier, so the harness exists.

##### THE SOUND SHOPPING LIST, and where to get it (Aaron asked 2026-08-07, answered 08-08)

**What the game has today, measured, not remembered.** `audio.js` synthesises
**eleven** effects from oscillators and noise: `click select net buzzer brick
steal tap whistle whoosh horn zap`. There are **zero** sound-effect FILES in the
repo. The only sourced audio is the soundtrack: eight Ketsa mp3s in
`docs/play/audio/`, CC BY 4.0, credited in the Rulebook.

**The line between synth and sourced is not taste, it is physics.** An
oscillator can fake a buzzer, a whistle and a rim ping, because those are simple
tones. It cannot fake anything made of MANY THINGS AT ONCE: a crowd, a shoe on
wood, a net, a ball on hardwood. Synthesised crowd noise sounds like a
hairdryer. Every item below is on the sourced side for that reason.

| Sound | Why it cannot be synthesised | Notes for the search |
|---|---|---|
| **Crowd cheer** (the one Aaron asked for) | hundreds of voices, no periodicity | 2 to 4s arena swell, **no music, no commentator**, clean tail. **Ideally 3 intensities** so a sweep can be louder than a finish |
| **Crowd groan / ooh** | same | 1 to 2s, for a miss on a big moment |
| **Sneaker squeak** | broadband stick-slip | short, dry, 2 or 3 variants so it does not loop-sound |
| **Ball on hardwood** | transient plus room tone | a dribble and a single bounce, separate |
| **Net swish (real)** | dozens of nylon strands | the synth `net` is fine for the Daily Five; a real one would lift a made bucket |
| **Rim clank** | metal ring with a long decay | pairs with the brick |

**Where to get them, in the order I would try.** The rule is the same one this
project already applies to facts: **prefer the licence you can quote.**

1. **Pixabay Sound Effects** (`pixabay.com/sound-effects/`). Free, **no
   attribution required**, commercial use allowed under their content licence.
   Start here because it is the only free source with no string attached to
   the file. Search: *arena crowd cheer*, *basketball squeak*, *sneaker squeak*.
2. **Freesound** (`freesound.org`). Enormous and high quality, but the licence
   is **per file**: filter to **CC0** and nothing else, or you inherit an
   attribution obligation on a game you may sell. Search: *crowd cheer indoor*,
   *gymnasium ambience*, *basketball bounce wood*.
3. **Zapsplat** (`zapsplat.com`). Free with attribution, or a small yearly fee
   to drop it. Good, consistent sports library, which matters: six sounds
   recorded by one person in one room sit together better than six from six
   sources.
4. **PAID, if the free ones sound thin.** `A Sound Effect`, `Boom Library`,
   `Soundsnap`. One-off purchase, written commercial licence, no attribution.
   A basketball pack is usually 20 to 60 dollars and would cover this whole
   table in one go. **This is the option I would actually recommend** if the
   game is ever going to take money, because it turns six licence questions
   into one receipt.

**AVOID:** the BBC Sound Effects library (free, but the licence is personal and
educational, NOT commercial, and this game may go commercial), YouTube rips, and
anything on a "free sounds" aggregator that does not name a licence on the page.
A sound with no licence is the audio version of a fact with no source.
