# Outreach draft — the Black Fives Foundation

**Status:** DRAFT for Aaron to edit and send. Nothing sent.
**To:** the Black Fives Foundation (contact form at blackfives.org) — founder Claude Johnson
**Why:** "Black Fives" and "Black Fives Era" are registered trademarks of Black Fives, Inc.
Their FAQ states the trademark but gives no usage guidance. We want to use the correct
historical term in a game that teaches this history, so we ask rather than assume.

**Framing note:** this is a permission request, not an apology. The game isn't public and
nobody has seen the old label, so there's nothing to make amends for — we simply want the
right name on the right history before anyone plays it.

**Before sending, decide:** are we asking for (a) permission to use the term as a feature
name, (b) a blessing plus a credit line, or (c) an actual collaboration? The draft below
asks for (a)+(b) and leaves the door open to (c).

## Draft

Subject: Permission request — using "Black Fives Era" in a basketball history game

Hello,

My name is Aaron Selkridge. I'm building a basketball game called Ball Knowledge, where
what makes you good at it is what you actually know about the game. It's a personal
project, not yet released.

A section of it covers early Black basketball — the New York Rens' 88-game streak, Bob
Douglas, Cumberland Posey's Loendi Big Five, Edwin Bancroft Henderson organizing the game
in 1904, Pop Gates and Dolly King integrating the NBL in 1946. Your work is where I
confirmed how this period is properly framed, and that the term for it is the Black Fives
Era.

I understand "Black Fives" and "Black Fives Era" are your registered trademarks, and that
you coined them through the research that recovered this history. I'd like your permission
to use "Black Fives Era" as the name of this category in the game, and to credit the Black
Fives Foundation on-screen with a link to blackfives.org so players can go to the source.
If you'd rather I use a different label, I'll use whatever you tell me — the history stays
in either case.

Most people who play this game will never have heard of the Rens. That's the reason I'd
rather ask for the real name than work around it.

If there's interest in going further than permission — reviewing the questions for
accuracy, or telling me what belongs in the set that I've missed — I'd welcome it.

Thank you for the work that made this findable.

Aaron Selkridge

[link to the game]
