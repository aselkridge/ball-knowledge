# Outreach draft — Sports Reference LLC

**Status:** DRAFT for Aaron to edit and send. **Nothing sent.**
**To:** the feedback form at `https://www.sports-reference.com/feedback/`
(no email address is published anywhere on the site; the form is the front door
they offer). Sean Forman is the founder and posts publicly about bot traffic.
**Why:** their terms bar using site content for *"prompting, or instructing
artificial intelligence models"* to generate *"answers, text, scores,
statistics"*. That describes how this project checks a card. **Aaron ruled
option C on 2026-08-07: ask them.**

**Framing note, and it decides the tone of the whole letter:** this is not a
confession and not a legal argument. It is a small project telling a site it
admires exactly what it does, and asking whether that is welcome. Sports
Reference's own terms already say sharing and repackaging data from individual
pages is *"welcomed, whether for commercial or non-commercial purposes"* as long
as they get credit. **We are almost certainly already inside their guiding
principle.** The letter exists to convert "almost certainly" into a written
answer from the only people who can give one.

## WHY THIS SITE AND NOT ANOTHER — measured 2026-08-07, not estimated

This is the number that decides how much the letter is worth.

| | |
|---|---|
| source pages on a Sports Reference domain | **517** of 1,783 (491 basketball-reference, 26 sports-reference) |
| facts in the bank citing them at all | 204 of 1,526 (13%) |
| **DEALABLE cards citing them at all** | **199 of 318 — 63%** |
| **DEALABLE cards citing them and NOTHING ELSE** | **128 of 318 — 40%** |
| of the 705 pages cited exactly once (the V32 mine) | 116 are theirs (16%) |

**Read the third and fourth rows twice.** Basketball-Reference is 13% of the raw
bank and **63% of everything that has actually passed verification.** That gap is
not an accident: it is the single best Tier 1 source for the checking work, so
the more the bank gets proved, the more it leans here. **Two in five shipping
cards would have no source at all if this door shut.**

And the dependency is still growing. Of the four verification blocks left, the
90-card block needs *one more publisher* per card and Basketball-Reference is the
obvious second publisher for most of them.

**So the honest summary: this is not one source among many. It is the backbone of
the verified pool, and the only one where a written yes would change what the
project can safely do.**

Worth stating the other side too. **It is not irreplaceable in principle.** The
underlying facts are free — nobody owns "Jordan won six titles" — and the same
facts exist in contemporaneous reporting, league record books, and Wikidata's
references. Replacing it would be slow and expensive, not impossible. That is the
difference between *needing* an answer and *wanting* one.

## THE DRAFT

> **Subject: A question about your terms, from a small basketball trivia project**
>
> Hello,
>
> I am building a small basketball trivia game as a personal project. It is not
> in an app store, there is no real money in it, and right now it is about to go
> out to twenty friends for feedback.
>
> The thing that makes it different from other trivia games is that every single
> question carries a source. Not a general credits page — each individual card
> records the URL it was proved against, how reliable that source is, and the
> date a person actually read it. Roughly 1,500 facts, and a great many of them
> point at Basketball-Reference, because when I need to be certain about
> something you are where I go.
>
> I read your Terms of Use properly rather than assuming, and I want to check one
> clause with you rather than guess.
>
> **What I do:** I open a page, read it, and write one trivia question from what
> it says, with a link back to that page attached to the question. Nothing is
> crawled, nothing is downloaded in bulk, and I am well inside your published
> rate guidance. I am not building anything anyone could use instead of your
> site: it is a game that asks "who led the league in rebounds in 1987", not a
> place to look up 1987.
>
> Your Permitted Use section says sharing and repackaging data found on
> individual pages is welcomed as long as you are credited, which is what I
> believe I am doing, and crediting you is something I actively want to do.
>
> **The clause I want to ask about** is the one prohibiting use of site content
> for "training, fine-tuning, prompting, or instructing artificial intelligence
> models". I build this project with an AI coding assistant, and part of how I
> check a fact is that the assistant reads the page with me and helps me confirm
> I have the number right before the question goes in. That is a verification
> step, not a generation step: nothing on your site becomes training data, and no
> question is invented from your content without a human reading the page.
>
> I genuinely could not tell from the wording whether that is what the clause is
> aimed at, and I would rather ask than assume. So:
>
> 1. Is per-fact citation of the kind I have described welcome, and is there a
>    particular attribution wording you would prefer I use?
> 2. Does the AI clause reach a person using an assistant to check a fact they
>    are reading, or is it aimed at bulk training and automated generation?
> 3. If there is a better way for a project like mine to do this — a rate,
>    a route, a form of words, or simply a no — I will follow it.
>
> I am not asking for a data feed and I am not asking for anything free that you
> sell. I am asking whether the way I am already working is acceptable to you,
> and I will change it if it is not.
>
> Thank you for the site. It is the reason a project like this is possible at
> all.
>
> Aaron
> [contact]

## NOTES FOR AARON BEFORE SENDING

**What a "no" would actually cost, so the downside is on the table before you
send.** If they say no to the AI clause question, the 128 cards resting on them
alone do not vanish — they are already proved and dated. What changes is method
going forward: reading their pages becomes a person-only job, which is slower.
It does not delete anything already banked.

**What a "yes" is worth.** It removes the single biggest open question hanging
over Track A, it unblocks V32 without a caveat, and it is the kind of thing worth
having in writing before twenty people become two hundred.

**Do not offer money and do not ask about pricing.** The $5,000 figure people
quote is for custom dataset exports, which is a completely different request and
would reframe this as a commercial negotiation. This is a question about
permitted use.

**Do not mention that nobody has ever been enforced against.** It is true, it is
in the research file, and saying it out loud converts a polite question into an
implied threat.

**One risk worth naming, and it is real:** asking makes you visible. Today they
have no idea this project exists. A "no" that would never have been enforced is
still a "no" once you have it in writing, and it binds a person who asked in a
way it does not bind the thousands who never did. **Claude's honest read: send it
anyway.** The clause is new, it is squarely aimed at how this project works, the
project's whole identity is that it shows its sources, and being the kind of
project that asks is consistent with being the kind of project that cites. But
this is Aaron's call and the downside deserves to be seen clearly first.

**Timing.** No rush. It blocks nothing on Track A: V32 is behind other work
anyway and the existing 318 cards are unaffected. Send it when it suits.
