The Rulebook

prototype rules · they evolve with the game

Two players, one screen. Orange attacks the right hoop, Blue attacks the left. First to 11.

On offense: tap one of your players to select them — their reachable tiles light up. Tap a tile to move, tap a teammate to pass, or hit SHOOT when you're in a scoring zone.

Shooting = answering. Close shots ask easy questions for 2. Mid-range asks medium for 2. Threes ask hard questions for 3. A wrong answer is a brick — live rebound.

Your knowledge level. Before tip-off the room picks a level — Casual · Rookie · Baller · Pro · Legend, or Surprise Me, which rolls a fresh difficulty on every single card. The level doesn't change WHAT you do, it slides HOW HARD every question is: a Rookie's three asks a medium question, a Legend's layup asks a hard one. In a handicap room each player sets their own, so a crew with wildly different basketball knowledge can still play a close game.

What your players actually do. A player's position is what matters on the floor today. Point guards cover the most ground and eat crossovers alive; centres protect the rim, get the easiest block cards in the paint and win tap-offs there — but they handle the ball like it's greased. Forwards sit in between. That's why the five slots feel different to play.

Star power isn't wired into the rules yet — on purpose. Right now a Superstar card and a Role card play identically; a stacked pack gets you a better stat sheet and bragging rights, not an edge. Player ratings are coming in a later update, and when they land they'll be a nudge, not a trump card — a great card should make you happy, never decide the game. Ball knowledge is the jumpshot. That's the whole point.

Defenders are real. A defender within one tile makes your shot question one tier harder — and even if you hit it, they get a block card (Centers get easier blocks in the paint). With a man marking you, retreating and sliding sideways is free — but advancing lights tiles red: a crossover challenge, easy for guards, brutal for big men, and if you flub it the defender still has to earn the steal with his own card — if he misses too, the move is simply wasted. Carrying a crossover 3+ tiles past your man is a deep cross — one tier harder, and those tiles glow a darker red so you know before you leap. Winning a cross still costs a step: you land one square short of where you aimed (when there's room). With two defenders in the gap, the duel is against the one closest to your driving line — the confirm prompt names him.

Crossovers are duels now. Hit your handle question and the defender gets his own card to stay in front — quick guards eat these, bigs on skates don't. Both right? ANKLE BATTLE — a tap-off, edge to the ball-handler. Contests read position too: a defender square in your chest jacks your shot a full tier; a diagonal closeout leaves you cleaner but hands him a tougher block card.

Confirm your moves. Tapping a tile or a teammate stages the action — nothing happens until you hit Confirm ✓, so a stray thumb can't burn a possession. Tapping a teammate asks: pass to him, or move him instead?

The release meter. Answer a shot (or a risky pass) right and a timing bar pops — tap to lock the sweeping marker. Dead center is perfect: a perfect release rises clean over any contest. The wide middle is good. The red edges shank it, right answer or not. Knowledge earns the look; touch finishes it.

Camera & controls: tapping a player leans the camera in on him; tap empty court to release him and pull back out. The ↺ button replays the last move — for when your opponent swears they didn't see it.

Go for the steal. On your defensive slide, pick a defender standing next to the ball-handler and hit Go for the steal: you answer a card (quick guards get easier ones), then the handler answers to protect the rock, and if you both hit it's a RIP OR GRIP tap-off (edge to the handler). Miss your card and the reach burns your slide.

Three in the key. Any offensive player camping the paint for 3 of your actions in a row is a whistle and a turnover — you'll get a ⚠ warning at 2. Keep bodies moving.

The timing bar belongs to the passer/shooter. When it pops it shows WHOSE finger it is in their team color — if it's not your color, hands off; there's nothing for the defense to do there.

Ball pressure is real. A defender within a tile of the ball-handler AND closer to the rim contests every forward or diagonal-forward pass (inbounds included) — sideways and backward passes stay free.

Only defenders in front of you contest shots. A man trailing behind the play can't affect your look — chase-down blocks arrive later as a signature skill.

Red tile logic: red tiles are PLAYABLE — tapping one starts a crossover challenge (a defender would have to be beaten: your marker, a helper on the line, or any tile behind a man). Beside/in front of a man, and all retreats, stay orange and free. The rim you're attacking glows in your color — that's "forward," however you've rotated the court.

Backcourt rule: once the ball crosses half, going back over is a live violation — those tiles glow dark red as a warning, and if you confirm it anyway the whistle blows: turnover. (An easy mode that blocks illegal moves outright rides with the coach tutorial.)

Rim tap-offs: if the shooter AND the blocker both answer right, it goes to a tap battle at the rim — rim-protecting Centers get the edge on layups.

Screens win games. Park an off-ball teammate next to a defender and that defender is screened — red lanes open back up. Watch the tiles change color as you set it.

Passing risk is about the lane, not just distance. Clean lane at medium range = free swing. A lurker near the lane = answer up. Full-court heaves are always hard.

Inbounds must be passed in. The inbounder can't move or shoot — but you may set up one cutter first (the defense gets a slide to answer), then tap a teammate to put it in play.

After each offensive action, the defense slides one player — up to one square less than his offensive speed (quick guards cover more ground than bigs). Defenders stranded in the backcourt sprint at full speed to get back. Transition matters.

Missed shots are live. Players near the rim battle for the board — a tap-race, orange taps left, blue taps right, closest player gets the box-out edge. Nobody near the rim? Long rebound, out of bounds, other team's ball.

Made buckets get inbounded under the rim, like real basketball — no teleporting.

Passing has range. Short passes are automatic. Cross-court lasers demand a medium question. A full-court heave is a hard question — sail it and it's out of bounds.

Drag the court to rotate it — and pinch to zoom. Tap still selects. Tile coordinates (A1-style) run along the edges, so you can call your shots like a chess player.

Whose hoop is whose: each rim wears its attacker's color — orange baseline, glow, and "ORANGE SCORES HERE" label on the hoop Orange attacks; blue on the other. The rim you're hunting also pulses in your color on your possession.

Clocks: the offense gets :24 to commit each action and the defense gets :12 to slide (the clock pauses while cards, meters, and battles run). Let it hit zero on offense and it's a shot-clock turnover; on defense, the slide is skipped. In 4 QUARTERS format, each quarter is 6 possessions — most points after Q4 wins, and a tie goes straight to sudden death.

SUDDEN DEATH: tie it up at game point (10–10 in a race to 11) and the board freezes — alternating trivia cards, scored-on team first. Both right or both wrong: next round, and the cards go hard. The first round where one team hits and the other misses ends the whole game.

Zones: green tiles = layup range · amber = mid-range · red = three-point land.

Dropped connection? If your phone refreshes or loses signal mid-game, the room is held ~45 seconds — you'll get a Reconnect button, and your friend sees "Opponent dropped — holding…" instead of the game just vanishing. Rejoin and the live board resyncs. Refresh the page entirely and it even offers to jump you back in.

Online (alpha): Main menu → Online. One of you creates a room and texts the 4-letter code; the other joins. Host is Orange and sets league, eras, and house rules — then both players get a squad check: see your five, shuffle until it feels right, lock it in. When both lock, the matchup screen faces the squads off and the tip goes up. You only see your own trivia cards — your opponent just sees you sweating on the clock. First connect can take ~30 seconds while the free server wakes up.

Music & settings: the ⚙ on the title screen (and Settings in the pause menu) controls the color theme, the music, sound FX, court labels, and motion — each phone keeps its own. The ♪ button is a quick music toggle. Music: "Funkorama", "Funk Game Loop", "Funky Chunk", "Hot Pursuit" & "Marty Gots a Plan" by Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), licensed under CC BY 4.0.
